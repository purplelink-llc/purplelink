## Option 1

I shipped a new kit this week: an automated clipping pipeline for Twitch and YouTube streams. Before it went live for purchase, I ran a red-team pass over the packaged source -- reading it as a buyer who has never seen the rest of my codebase.

Found seven problems. One file hardcoded two real Facebook Page IDs from my own internal setup. Another file the publisher depended on was missing entirely, which would have made the publisher unusable on a fresh install. A machine-specific path to my own yt-dlp installation was in the code. iCloud sync was writing into the buyer's iCloud Drive on every run without asking. Filenames derived from user-supplied YouTube URLs were not sanitized.

I removed the Facebook ID file entirely, added the missing file, replaced the machine path with an environment variable, made the iCloud sync opt-in, and sanitized the filename handling.

The product copy also changed: the original claimed a longform compiler that stitches clips into one video. That module was gone, so the claim came out.

The kit is at purplelink.llc/kits/clip-pipeline/

It took about two hours to audit. Shipping code to a paying stranger who is going to trust that it works is a different obligation than shipping to yourself.

If you sell code products, I am curious what your pre-ship review looks like.

## Option 2

I built a daily traffic dashboard this week that reads first-party analytics from purplelink.llc and getmuscleonglp.com and renders an HTML file I open as a local bookmark.

The straightforward part was pulling the numbers and displaying them. The part I spent more time on was a single decision: when to suppress the week-over-week comparison.

My first-party analytics beacon went live at different dates on each page and tool. If you calculate week-over-week growth during the two weeks after a beacon goes live, the numbers look like explosive growth. Page X went from 0 to 120 visits. Technically true. Measuring when the beacon was installed, not actual growth in traffic to the page.

The dashboard suppresses week-over-week for any URL where the baseline window predates the tracking start date. It shows a blank slot rather than a number that would mislead.

The dashboard runs at 9am via a launchd agent on my Mac and falls back to a cached copy of the script when my external drive is unmounted, so an unplugged drive does not skip a day. The data never leaves my machine.

It is internal tooling, so not published. But the design decision is the same one I try to apply to Purplelink's public stats page: prefer an empty slot to a number that tells a story the data does not support.

If you track your own product metrics, I am curious how you handle the baseline problem.

## Option 3

I published a guide this week on generating a journal cover letter from a manuscript abstract.

The cover letter is required by most journals and is almost entirely boilerplate in structure: what the paper does, why it fits the scope of this journal specifically, what methodological or empirical contribution it makes, and three or four confirmation statements about originality, concurrent submission, and author agreement. The specific wording of those statements varies by journal.

Most researchers write this from scratch at the end of a submission process that is already long. The guide walks through what each section is meant to accomplish, which parts are factual -- pulled directly from the abstract or the acknowledgements -- and which require a judgment call about fit and contribution.

The Purplelink tool takes the abstract and the target journal name as input and structures the letter around those. The researcher still needs to fill in the confirmation statements and verify the journal-specific framing.

The guide is honest about what the tool does not cover: it cannot assess whether your paper actually fits a given journal's scope, and it does not know a journal's specific word limit for the letter itself. Those require a read of the journal's author instructions.

Guide is at purplelink.llc/guides/generate-journal-cover-letter/

If you have submitted to many journals, I am curious whether your approach to the cover letter has changed over time.
