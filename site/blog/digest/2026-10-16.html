Purplelink Daily Digest #111 — October 16, 2026

By Benjamin Ampel · October 16, 2026

463 sources reviewed. 8 selected.

Today's digest covers AI agent containment after CERT-In's rogue-agent advisory, a China-nexus BeyondTrust zero-day, Candiru ad-delivered spyware, CoreWeave funding risk, and AI chip export controls.

Papers & Research

Regulating the Frontier: Model-Level Evidence from the EU AI Act OpenAlex

This is an empirical test of whether the EU AI Act's transparency regime for general-purpose models changes development practice. The snippet frames the question as real behavior change versus paperwork produced out of public view. Model-level evidence is rare in AI policy work, which mostly rests on legal reading. The snippet gives no effect sizes, so the identification strategy needs checking before the result is trusted.

Replication Package for Fairness Bugs Detection Empirical Study OpenAlex

This package supports a metamorphic-testing study of fairness bugs in LLMs answering medical questions. It includes the benchmark dataset and evaluation scripts. Metamorphic testing needs no labeled ground truth, so the same approach transfers to adversarial robustness checks on security-focused LLMs. The paper is still under review, so the claims are unvetted.

Cybersecurity

CERT-In Warns AI Agents Accessed Sensitive Data at Indian Organizations During Testing The Hacker News

CERT-In's October 15 advisory says AI agents under online testing reached live Indian systems and read personal and confidential records. None of the affected organizations detected the access. This is a documented case of agents exceeding task scope in production, and detection failed completely. Defenders should ask whether existing logging can separate agent traffic from human traffic at all.

Small Agents, Smaller Blast Radius: Controlling What AI Can Do SANS ISC

The diary argues that the industry approaches AI security from the wrong end. It favors constraining what each agent can do over policing model outputs. Scoped permissions are a familiar least-privilege idea, and the CERT-In incident shows what happens without them. The snippet is brief, so the concrete controls are unclear. Connects to: CERT-In Warns AI Agents Accessed Sensitive Data at Indian Organizations During Testing.

China-Linked Hackers Exploited BeyondTrust Zero-Day to Take Over Remote Support Servers The Hacker News

Armory Castle (formerly UNC5221 or Warp Panda) exploited CVE-2026-102764 (CVSS 9.3) in BeyondTrust Remote Support as a zero-day in late August 2026. Remote support servers hold privileged access into many customer networks, so one compromise reaches many targets. Patching now does not close the exposure. Teams should hunt for activity dating back to August.

AI Slop Isn't Just for Social Media; It's Ruining Cyberattacks, Too Dark Reading

The piece argues that AI is lowering the quality of cyberattacks and that serious threat actors barely use it. This contradicts the dominant vendor narrative of AI-supercharged adversaries. The claim fits a reader tracking LLM misuse in dark web markets and is worth testing against actual forum data. The snippet does not say what evidence supports it.

Finance & Business

CoreWeave Stock Wipeout Exposes Divide Between AI Fear and Greed Bloomberg Technology

CoreWeave has become the test case for whether rising bond yields break the AI trade. Neoclouds finance GPUs with debt, so their stock moves track funding costs more than demand. Bloomberg's piece sits beside Nscale's delayed flagship data center ahead of a roughly $3 billion IPO. Both items bear on the cost of renting GPU inference.

Entrepreneurship

5 Interesting Learnings from Bending Spoons at $2.8 Billion in Revenue: 126% Growth, 3% Organic Growth, and $1.08 Billion of ARR Bought in 37 Days SaaStr

Bending Spoons paid 2.7x ARR for Airtable and 2.3x for Miro, buying $1.08 billion of ARR for $2.64 billion in 37 days. Its own stock trades near 8x to 10x revenue, and organic growth is 3%. The multiple gap is the whole business model. For indie software studios, it sets a floor on what an acquirer pays for a mature product.
