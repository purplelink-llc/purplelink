Science reference format

Science numbers references and notes in the order they are cited and puts the citation numbers in italic parentheses, such as (18, 19). Every author is listed: the instructions say not to use “et al.”

Checked 2026-10-01 against the sources linked at the end of this page.

What Science expects

  • Reference style: References and Notes are numbered in the order cited: main text first, then text boxes, captions, notes and acknowledgments, then supplementary materials. One combined list.
  • In-text form: Citation numbers in parentheses, italicized: (18, 19), (18–20), (18, 20–22).
  • Authors: Initials first, family names in full, separated by commas with no “and”. The full list of authors is required; do not use et al., op. cit. or ibid.
  • Journal articles: Title, then journal in italics, volume in bold, full page range with an en dash, and year in parentheses.
  • Manuscript format: Word (.docx) is preferred. LaTeX authors are asked to convert to .docx, or to use the Science LaTeX template and upload a PDF.
  • Front matter: Title of no more than 96 characters including spaces; abstract of 125 words or less.

Setting up the bibliography

Science's LaTeX page recommends BibTeX over hand-coded \bibitem entries. The template zip contains science_template.tex and science_template.bib: rename both, put your references in the .bib file, and cite with plain \cite{}.

Keep the .aux and .bbl files from compilation. For a revised version, Science needs the .bbl contents, either pasted into the .tex file or uploaded with it. Do not add packages beyond those the template already loads.

Three references in Science citation style

  1. M. Jinek, K. Chylinski, I. Fonfara, M. Hauer, J. A. Doudna, E. Charpentier, A programmable dual-RNA–guided DNA endonuclease in adaptive bacterial immunity. Science 337, 816–821 (2012). https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1225829
  2. G. E. Hinton, R. R. Salakhutdinov, Reducing the dimensionality of data with neural networks. Science 313, 504–507 (2006). https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1127647
  3. G. Hardin, The tragedy of the commons. Science 162, 1243–1248 (1968). https://doi.org/10.1126/science.162.3859.1243

LaTeX template

Science provides a Word template and a LaTeX template. The LaTeX one is science_template_v1.1.zip (version 1.1, updated April 2026) on the Science LaTeX page. We do not have a Science template page yet.

Common mistakes

  • Using et al. in the reference list. Science asks for the complete list of authors.
  • Writing “and” before the last author. Names are separated by commas only.
  • A separate reference list for the supplement. Science wants a single list, with supplement-only references numbered last.
  • Adding LaTeX packages to the template. The LaTeX page says extra packages break the conversion software and cause delays.
  • Citations or abbreviations in the abstract.

Frequently asked

What reference style does Science use?

A numbered style. References and notes are numbered in the order cited, and citation numbers go in italic parentheses, such as (18, 19).

Does Science allow et al. in references?

No. The instructions say not to use et al. in place of the complete list of authors' names.

Can I submit to Science in LaTeX?

Yes, but Word is preferred. Science asks LaTeX authors to convert to .docx if possible, or to use its LaTeX template and upload a PDF.
