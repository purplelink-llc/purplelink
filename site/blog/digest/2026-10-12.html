Purplelink Daily Digest #107 — October 12, 2026

By Benjamin Ampel · October 12, 2026

584 sources reviewed. 7 selected.

AI-assisted attacks using Claude Code and ARTEX AI hit Ukrainian and South Korean targets. Also in the digest: the DarkSword P7 iOS exploit kit, a Zod npm compromise report, EU AI Act model-level evidence, and Tencent's AI capex bond.

Papers & Research

Regulating the Frontier: Model-Level Evidence from the EU AI Act OpenAlex

This paper asks an empirical question most AI-policy commentary skips: does the EU AI Act's GPAI transparency regime change how models are built, or only produce paperwork? Model-level evidence is rarer and more useful than legal analysis of the text. The snippet is truncated, so effect sizes and the identification strategy need checking in the full paper.

Replication Package for Fairness Bugs Detection Empirical Study OpenAlex

A replication package for a metamorphic-testing study of fairness bugs in LLMs on medical QA, including benchmark data and evaluation scripts. Metamorphic testing needs no ground-truth labels, a technique that transfers to adversarial robustness testing of security LLMs. The paper is still under review, so treat the findings as preliminary.

Cybersecurity

CyberXero Uses Claude Code in Campaign That Stole Over 628,000 Ukrainian Records The Hacker News

CyberXero paired commodity offensive tooling such as Cobalt Strike with Claude Code in a two-pronged initial access campaign that exfiltrated over 628,000 Ukrainian records. The notable detail is the mix: agentic AI is being bolted onto old tradecraft against WordPress, e-commerce, and critical infrastructure targets rather than replacing it. Researchers studying LLM-enabled threat detection should ask which artifacts of the AI-assisted workflow, such as prompt-shaped scripts or tooling cadence, are detectable.

ARTEX AI, Claude agents used in cyberattacks on South Korean banks Bleeping Computer

A single Chinese operator reportedly used the ARTEX AI penetration testing suite plus Claude agents to disrupt the South Korean financial sector. A lone actor with off-the-shelf AI pentest tooling producing sector-wide impact is the strongest real-world data point yet for the offensive-agent scaling argument. Attribution rests on reporting, so the open question is how much of the impact came from the agents versus ordinary access. Connects to: CyberXero Uses Claude Code in Campaign That Stole Over 628,000 Ukrainian Records.

P7 DarkSword iOS Exploit Kit Adds Crypto Wallet Data Theft and Remote Commands The Hacker News

The P7 variant of DarkSword shrinks its on-device footprint while adding keychain and crypto-wallet theft and two-way C2. The smaller footprint is the signal: it targets the on-device forensic artifacts that mobile defenders rely on. Apple-platform developers should note the keychain theft, which raises the stakes for how apps store secrets.

Zod 4.6.6 compromised by malware? Hacker News

A reported malware compromise of Zod 4.6.6 would hit one of the most widely depended-on validation libraries in the TypeScript ecosystem. Libraries at this depth turn a single publish into a broad supply-chain event. The claim is a question mark in the title, so verify against the advisory and lockfiles before treating it as confirmed.

Finance & Business

Tencent Tells Investors Its $5 Billion Bond Mainly for AI Capex Bloomberg Technology

Tencent is raising about $5 billion in debt, mainly for AI capex. Debt-funded rather than cash-flow-funded buildout is a meaningful shift in AI infrastructure economics. Paired with the Firmus IPO collapse, it suggests capital markets are splitting between incumbents who can borrow and new entrants who cannot.
