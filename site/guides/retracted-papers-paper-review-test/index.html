Does an AI paper review find what later gets a paper retracted? A test on 23 papers

By Benjamin Ampel · October 10, 2026

I make Paper Review, a paid pre-submission check for manuscripts. This is my own test of it, not an independent one. It pointed at the documented problem in 14 of 23 papers, partly in 4, and missed 5.

How the test worked

  1. I selected open-access papers whose retraction notices, found through Crossref's retraction records (which include the Retraction Watch data), give a specific reason that could in principle be seen in the manuscript. Fabrication and misconduct cases were left out. Each paper carries a licence that allows reuse.
  2. I took the full text of each paper from Europe PMC's open XML service and typeset it. Its PDF service sits behind a bot check, which I did not bypass. The typeset versions have no figures, and some tables and equations were lost in conversion.
  3. Each paper went through the production review (Standard tier, general field) with no hint that it was retracted.
  4. A separate model read only the notice's reason and the review's findings, not the paper, and answered yes, partly or no. I read every yes, partly and no against the report.

Result

Of 23 papers, the review pointed at the documented problem in 14 and partly in 4. It did not in 5. I count 11 or 12 of the 14 as clear matches. Two are generous: one review matched only half of its notice, and one notice is too vague to confirm. Where the notice itself said the error was visible in the manuscript, the review pointed at it in 9 of 11 papers. Where that was uncertain, it did in 5 of 12.

Some of the clear matches: too few events per variable for the number of predictors, a median below the minimum in a table, electron-volt values converted to kcal/mol with the wrong factor, odds ratios that were the reciprocal of what the counts imply, and a confidence interval that crossed zero beside a claim of significance.

What it missed

Two of the five misses are partly the test's fault: my conversion dropped a table in one paper and rendered another paper's equations unreadable (the review said so). Two notices describe clerical or record-handling mistakes, such as data from the wrong wave merged into a file, which the published text does not show. One is a plain miss: a paper excluded pauses and unvoiced segments from an acoustic analysis, and the review did not question it.

Is it just flagging everything?

A fair worry, because a report lists about seven high-confidence concerns on almost any paper. I ran six papers that were not retracted. Their reports averaged 6.5 such concerns, against 6.4 for the retracted set, so the count tells you nothing about whether a paper has a problem. The content does. I gave the same judge each retraction reason together with a report written for an unrelated paper: it said yes in 2 of 46 cases, against 14 of 23 for the matching reports. Both of those two were real overlaps; one unrelated paper really does have too few events per variable.

I did not check whether the concerns raised on the non-retracted papers are correct. They are real published papers with real flaws, and six is too few to give a false-alarm rate.

Limits

  • The 23 papers were chosen for retractions with a specific, checkable cause, mostly from PLOS ONE, Scientific Reports and Cureus. Subtle flaws and misconduct are not represented, and those are harder to find.
  • One model scored the results, and each paper was run once, so the percentages are not tight.
  • Figures were absent, so the figure scan was not exercised.
  • The review reads only what the paper shows. It cannot catch a mistake in data the paper does not include.
  • This says nothing about matching a human reviewer, and I do not claim it does.

Cost

Model fees were about $3.90 per paper, up to $5.40 for papers of up to 58 pages. The Standard review costs $9. The whole test, including the non-retracted papers and the baseline, cost about $113.

The papers

Every paper in the test is listed, including the misses. Each is a public retraction; follow the DOI to the paper and its notice. The last two columns are the judge's verdict and the finding it relied on.

23 retracted papers and what the review said about each
Paper Journal Reason in the retraction notice Review pointed at it Finding the judge relied on
10.1371/journal.pone.0140494 PloS one Regression model was overfitted: too few events per variable for the number of predictors, so the statistical conclusions were not supported. Yes The multivariable logistic model is overfit with quasi-separation: with 23 events and 5 covariates (≈4.6 events per variable), inflated ORs with huge CIs; predictor list statistically unreliable.
10.1097/md.0000000000006042 Medicine Confidence intervals in Table 2 were wrong because the wrong mode was chosen in the statistical software, making the reported odds ratios and intervals inconsistent. No None relevant
10.1186/s12884-023-05747-7 BMC pregnancy and childbirth Pooled estimate's 95% CI crossed zero, yet the text claimed a significant effect; birthweights were also compared without adjusting for gestational age. Partly Conclusion claims sildenafil 'prolongs pregnancies' but the pooled GA MD of 0.82 (95% CI [-0.15, 1.80], P = 0.10) is non-significant. The review never mentions birthweight being compared without adjusting for gestational age.
10.7759/cureus.50212 Cureus Impossible hematology values (hemoglobin 116 g/dL, neutrophil % above 100) and mutually incompatible model metrics (AUC below 0.5 with high accuracy and sensitivity). Yes Physiologically impossible values remain in the modelling data: Table 2 lists Neutrophil (%) max 6201 and Hemoglobin max 116 g/dL; SVM comorbidities AUC 0.45 and impossible metric combinations flagged.
10.1371/journal.pone.0304589 PloS one Table 2 showed a median below the minimum for one group; a core variable was missing, so analyses could not be reproduced. Yes Table 2 descriptive statistics are mathematically impossible: Profitability row places an apparent median below the minimum; regenerate Table 2; also supply raw data/code since the data availability statement is inaccurate.
10.1155/2018/1837634 BioMed research international Fundamental error in the calculations underlying the pooled results, raised by outside readers. Yes Multi-arm trials split into 14 comparisons sharing one placebo group (unit-of-analysis error), plus Lewis/Karlowski double-counted; all pooled weights, CIs and P-values invalid and must be re-run.
10.1371/journal.pone.0229035 PloS one Non-significant results from a very small sample were read as evidence of no effect; reported intervals actually indicated substantial effects of stiffness and height. Yes Underpowered null results (n = 5) are presented as 'no effect'; the paper's own tables show height and stiffness spreads as large as the significant shape effect. Fix: report effects with 95% CIs, run equivalence tests.
10.1371/journal.pone.0247992 PloS one Reported prevalences disagreed with the source survey data and with the paper's own odds ratios; a funnel plot was misused for non-meta-analytic data. Yes Country prevalences contradict the paper's own regression: 'highest modern contraceptive utilization in Comoros (61.49%)' is irreconcilable with Table 3's Comoros OR of 0.22 vs Burundi; the ranking is arithmetically impossible.
10.7759/cureus.40492 Cureus Physiologically implausible mean serum calcium levels, sample-size discrepancies and inconsistent reporting throughout. Yes Calcium mean 5.07 with range 2.07-12.39 and no units fits neither mg/dL nor mmol/L (5.07 mmol/L would be lethal). Methods state n=100 while the tables say n=121. Internal contradictions run throughout.
10.1177/1073274820976671 Cancer control : journal of the Moffitt Cancer Center Contradictory statements on survival and expression direction, inconsistent mutation reporting, and correlation statistics that did not fit the stated sample size. Yes Review flags survival direction contradicting between text and Figure 2C caption, the abstract reversing tumor-vs-normal expression, 1,692 mutated vs 0.1% frequency, and r=0.385/p=0.0052 impossible with n=7.
10.1371/journal.pone.0196128 PloS one Data extraction errors found by comparing the analysis dataset with the source trials (arms, participants, duration); some trials violated stated inclusion criteria. Yes Inclusion criterion 'treatment duration greater than two weeks' contradicted by 'duration of the interventions ranged from 1 week to 24 weeks', one-week trials violate the stated eligibility rule.
10.1371/journal.pone.0255392 PloS one Stress-scale items needing reverse coding appear to have been scored without it; figure wave labels were switched and tables disagreed with the dataset. Yes PSS monotonicity claim fails unless items 4, 5, 7, 8 are reverse-scored; no reverse-scoring is stated, so the plain sum described is invalid as a stress measure.
10.1039/d3ra08796d RSC advances Simulation setup described with mistakes (misused terms), and Table 4 values could not be obtained from the stated formulae. Yes MD section must be redone: engine, Hamiltonian, ensemble unspecified, and 'NVE cannot hold temperature fixed'. Table 4 is flagged as corrupted (wrong orbital labels, 1178 kcal/mol E(2)), and tabulated values do not follow from the stated equations.
10.1038/s41598-023-38091-z Scientific reports Errors in energy unit conversion (eV to kcal/mol), thermodynamic calculations, free energy correction and Boltzmann constant units made table values inaccurate. Yes Table 1 eV entries equal kcal/mol ÷ 27.21 instead of ÷ 23.06; thermodynamic ΔH/ΔG values physically impossible; conductivity expression gives Boltzmann's constant in kcal/mol while Eg is in eV.
10.1371/journal.pone.0194582 PloS one Internal tension force was miscalculated using the wrong displacement in two equations, invalidating the drill-string results in Figs 4-11. No None relevant
10.1371/journal.pone.0210555 PloS one Acoustic analyses failed to exclude pauses and unvoiced segments, and pitch tracking had errors; reanalysis undermined the voice-acoustics conclusions. No None relevant
10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01333 Frontiers in psychology Clerical error merging longitudinal waves assigned wave 3 data to the wrong participants; reanalysis changed the results pattern. No None relevant
10.1155/2021/8837319 Journal of obesity SAS code mistake classified most participants as one year older in Table 3; corrected estimates lost significance in one subgroup. Partly Table 3 HWC ≥65 stratum: RR 1.12 (1.00–1.27) marked P<0.05, but recomputed p ≈ 0.06–0.08; the key age-stratified positive result is borderline. Also flags tested-sample median age mismatch (69 vs 71).
10.7759/cureus.52054 Cureus Spreadsheet treated a minus sign as a formula, corrupting HbA1c medians and SDs, plus an error selecting pre-intervention values. Partly The review flags an undefined HbA1c sign convention and asks to verify that every study was entered with a consistent sign. It also asks how pre-post designs and time points were handled, but never identifies the spreadsheet corruption.
10.1371/journal.pone.0116682 PloS one Experimental and control groups were inadvertently switched during analysis, reversing the reported association. Yes HCC-vs-control odds ratios appear to be reciprocals of what the paper's own counts imply (GA crude 1.80 vs reported 0.54), pointing to a coding/reference-category error that reverses the protective claim.
10.1371/journal.pone.0278333 PloS one Analysis used 422 records when 182 should have been removed (unmatched IDs, out-of-range ages, absent children); sample size was wrong. No None relevant
10.1371/journal.pone.0223360 PloS one Coding error in p-value calculation affected a figure; corrected analysis showed no causal relationship in the validation data. Partly Table 3 has impossible or duplicated Λ entries (m=1000: 61.60 exceeds the 31.59 ceiling; m=20000 duplicates another row), so Fig 4 must be regenerated. The headline causal conclusion is sign-reversed and should be corrected or withdrawn.
10.21037/tcr.2020.01.32 Translational cancer research Authors found obvious errors in the statistical analysis and a graph and asked for retraction. Yes Multivariate Cox results are internally impossible: HDAC8 CI 0.215–6.416 spans 1 yet P=0.003; CDKN2A HR 0.65 lies outside its CI 0.680–1.468; P values duplicate log-rank values.
