Best PDF apps for Mac for academics (2026): 7 compared

By Benjamin Ampel · November 26, 2026

Most academics on a Mac can read, highlight and annotate papers for free. Pay for a PDF app when you need one specific tool (comparing versions, OCR, notes exported to Markdown), and pick the app by that tool.

Disclosure up front: I make Mac apps for academics, including ModernTex, a LaTeX editor that appears in the LaTeX editors guide linked below. None of the seven apps compared here is mine. I read every price and version on the vendors' own pages on November 26, 2026. Prices are in US dollars and leave out tax. They change, so check the linked page before you buy.

The short answer

Preview is free, comes with every Mac, and highlights, adds notes and redacts. Skim is the free choice for LaTeX writers, because it syncs the PDF with your source. Zotero's built-in reader is the free choice if Zotero already holds your library. Highlights Pro ($29.99 a year) exports your notes as Markdown. PDF Expert ($79.99 a year or $139.99 once for the Mac) edits text and runs OCR (optical character recognition, which turns a scanned page into text). Acrobat Pro ($19.99 a month on an annual plan) is the one to buy when someone asks you to compare two versions of a PDF or to work with Acrobat's own tools.

Pick this one if

  • You only read and highlight: Preview. It is already on your Mac.
  • You write in LaTeX: Skim, for SyncTeX between the PDF and your editor.
  • Your library lives in Zotero: Zotero's reader, so notes keep their citations.
  • You want notes in Obsidian or another Markdown app: Highlights Pro.
  • You edit PDFs or scan old papers: PDF Expert, lifetime licence if you only use a Mac.
  • A co-author or office needs Acrobat's tools: Acrobat Pro, on the plan your university pays for if it has one.

Each app in more detail

Preview

Preview cannot edit a PDF's text, but its Markup toolbar highlights, underlines, adds notes and speech bubbles, draws shapes and redacts. Apple's guide says a redaction can change while the file is open and becomes permanent once you close it. Table of contents entries that link to redacted text stay in the file, so check those before you send a redacted PDF. Preview also combines PDFs and password-protects them. To keep annotations editable, save with File, Save or Export. To flatten them, print to PDF (Annotate a PDF in Preview). If you are removing author names before double-blind review, the PDF metadata guide covers what redaction leaves behind.

Acrobat Reader

Reader is Adobe's free app for viewing, printing, signing and commenting, with sticky notes, highlights, strikethrough and freehand drawing (Acrobat Reader). It is worth having when a form only fills correctly in Adobe's own app. For reading papers, Preview does the same job without the download.

Acrobat Standard, Pro and Studio

All three plans include 100 GB of cloud storage. Standard leaves out comparing two versions of a PDF, redaction and the full set of more than 70 PDF tools. Pro adds them. Studio is Pro plus Adobe's AI Assistant (Acrobat plans). Pro is the Acrobat plan with the compare tool. It helps when a journal sends back a typeset proof and you want to check it against the version you submitted.

PDF Expert

PDF Expert edits text and images in a PDF, runs OCR, translates and signs. The yearly plan ($79.99) covers iPhone, iPad and Mac, and the lifetime licence ($139.99) covers the Mac only. Both come with a 30-day money-back guarantee and need macOS Monterey or later (PDF Expert pricing). Readdle lists a student and educator offer on the same page. I left its size out because the page did not state it.

Skim

Skim describes itself as a PDF reader and note-taker for scientific papers. It adds notes, highlights and snapshots (cropped views of a page you can keep open beside the text), exports notes as text, and has a reading bar and a presentation mode. Its LaTeX, SyncTeX and PDFSync support lets you click in the PDF to reach the source line, and it works with the BibDesk reference manager. Version 1.7.20 needs macOS 10.13 or later (Skim), and its SourceForge page lists the BSD licence. The SyncTeX guide shows how to connect Skim to an editor, and the LaTeX editors guide compares the editors on the other side.

Highlights

The free tier, Essentials, reads and annotates and exports notes as a PDF. Pro ($4.99 a month or $29.99 a year) exports notes as Markdown, HTML or TextBundle and sends them to other apps. It also adds Smart OCR, citation lookup, reference-manager integration and MathJax for equations. One subscription covers iPhone, iPad and Mac, with Family Sharing and a free trial (Highlights pricing).

Zotero

Zotero is a free, open-source reference manager from an independent nonprofit, and its download page lists Zotero 10 for Mac (Zotero). Its built-in reader turns highlights into notes that keep a link to the page and a citation you can drop into Word, LibreOffice or Google Docs. Two cautions come from Zotero's own documentation. First, the annotations live in Zotero's database, so another PDF app will not see them unless you export a PDF with the annotations embedded. Second, adding, deleting, reordering or rotating pages in another app puts existing annotations in the wrong place (Zotero PDF reader). If you cite from Zotero into LaTeX, the Zotero and LaTeX guide covers the export.

Three more worth a look

MarginNote 5 and LiquidText are free downloads on the Mac App Store with paid plans inside the app. MarginNote links PDF highlights to mind-map cards, and LiquidText pulls excerpts into a workspace beside the page. Each lists several in-app plans on the App Store, so check the listing for the plan you need. Sioyek is an open-source reader (GPL-3.0 licence) with SyncTeX, jumps to references and figures, and searchable highlights (Sioyek). None of the three is in the tables because I compared only apps whose prices fit a single row.

Frequently asked

What is the best free PDF app for academics on a Mac?

Start with Preview, which comes with macOS and highlights, adds notes and redacts. Add Skim if you write in LaTeX or want notes you can export as text, and use Zotero's built-in reader if Zotero already holds your library. All three are free.

Do I need Adobe Acrobat Pro as an academic?

Most readers do not. Acrobat Pro adds comparing two versions of a PDF, redaction and more than 70 PDF tools over Standard. It is US$19.99 a month on an annual plan, billed monthly, as listed on November 26, 2026. Buy it when a grant office, journal or co-author needs one of those tools.

Can Preview redact a PDF?

Yes. Apple's guide lists a Redaction Selection tool in Preview's Markup toolbar. You can change a redaction while the file is open, and it becomes permanent once you close the document. Apple notes that table of contents entries linking to redacted text are not removed.

Which PDF reader works with LaTeX on a Mac?

Skim. Its page lists LaTeX, SyncTeX and PDFSync support, so it can jump between a line in the PDF and the source line in your editor. It is free and BSD licensed, and version 1.7.20 needs macOS 10.13 or later.

Do Zotero annotations show up in other PDF apps?

Not by default. Zotero says annotations made in its built-in reader are stored in the Zotero database, so other PDF readers will not see them unless you export a PDF with the annotations embedded.
