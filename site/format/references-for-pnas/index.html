PNAS reference format

PNAS references are numbered in the order they appear in the text. Lists with more than five authors give the first author and “et al.”, and the LaTeX templates use pnas-new.bst.

Checked 2026-10-01 against the sources linked at the end of this page.

What PNAS expects

  • Reference style: PNAS style, numbered in the order references appear in the text. Each entry includes the full article title.
  • Authors: If there are more than five authors, list the first author's name followed by et al.
  • Journal names: MEDLINE/PubMed abbreviations, or the full title for journals not indexed in MEDLINE.
  • BibTeX style: pnas-new.bst, “the current PNAS bibliography style”.
  • Templates: Word and LaTeX templates for research articles, Brief Reports and the SI Appendix. The LaTeX templates are on Overleaf.
  • Length: The preferred length of a Research Report is 6 pages, with a maximum of 12. A standard 6-page article is about 4,000 words, 50 references and 4 medium-size figures or tables.

Setting up the bibliography

In the PNAS template, the bibliography style is:

\bibliographystyle{pnas-new}
\bibliography{refs}

PNAS also suggests the Automatic Journal Title Abbreviation package in Overleaf, so references do not appear longer than they will in print. References cited only in the SI go in a separate list.

LaTeX template

PNAS links its Word and Overleaf templates from the submission guidelines. Support for the LaTeX templates is through Overleaf. We do not have a PNAS template page yet.

Common mistakes

  • Listing six or more authors in full. Past five, PNAS gives the first author and et al.
  • Leaving out article titles. PNAS asks for the full title of each cited article.
  • One combined list for the main text and the SI. PNAS wants a separate citation list for references cited in the SI.
  • Citing unpublished meeting abstracts or “data not shown”. Neither is permitted.
  • Spelled-out journal names where a MEDLINE abbreviation exists.

Frequently asked

What bibliography style does PNAS use?

pnas-new.bst. The submission guidelines call it the current PNAS bibliography style.

When does PNAS use et al. in references?

When a reference has more than five authors, list the first author's name followed by et al.

How long is a PNAS research article?

The preferred length is 6 pages and the maximum is 12. PNAS describes a 6-page article as about 4,000 words, 50 references and 4 medium-size graphical elements.
