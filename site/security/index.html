Security and data handling

What happens to a file you give us, product by product.

Written for the person at a research office, IT department or ethics board who has to sign off before a lab can use these tools. Every statement here describes what the code does today. The privacy policy is the legal document. This page is the plain summary. Last reviewed September 30, 2026.

By product

What each product does with your files

Free tools that run in your browser
Reference Converter, the reference format pages, LaTeX Table Generator, Thesis Format Checker, Abstract Checker, the response letter template and the Submission Checklist run entirely in your browser. The text or PDF you give them never leaves your computer. The Citation Generator formats in your browser, but looks up the identifier you type: DOIs go from your browser to Crossref, ISBNs to Open Library, and arXiv IDs through our server to the arXiv API. Settings and checklist ticks are kept in your browser's local storage.
Free tools that run on our server
LaTeX to PDF, LaTeX Diff, LaTeX to Word, Word to LaTeX, Markdown to PDF, File to Markdown, PDF to Structured Data, PDF compression, BibTeX Builder, BibTeX Validator, Equation Renderer and the file-upload path of the Word Counter send your file over HTTPS to our backend. The backend runs on Modal (a cloud hosting platform). The file is processed in a temporary directory inside the container and discarded when the response is sent. No copy is written to durable storage or to logs. BibTeX Builder sends the DOI or arXiv ID you enter to CrossRef or arXiv. BibTeX Validator sends reference DOIs and titles to CrossRef and Semantic Scholar only when you tick those checks.
Paper Review and the paid checks
Paper Review, Anonymity Check, Citation Gap Analysis, Revision Review, Response to Reviewers and Resume Review run on Modal. The manuscript is sent to Anthropic's Claude API for analysis. Under Anthropic's commercial terms, inputs are retained for up to 30 days for abuse monitoring and are not used for model training. Reference titles and DOIs go to CrossRef, and the DOIs or titles of the references whose abstracts are checked against your claims go to OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar. Nothing from your manuscript's own text goes to those three. The result waits in Modal storage until you open it. For Paper Review, the report is also emailed as a PDF when the review finishes, through Resend, to the address on the Stripe receipt or the one entered at upload. The manuscript and the annotated PDF are never emailed. Purplelink deletes the manuscript, the online copy of the review and the annotated PDF within about a day after the result is first opened, or within 48 hours if it never is. A cleanup job runs once a day to enforce these limits. The free reference check on the Paper Review page reads the PDF on Modal, sends reference titles and DOIs to CrossRef, and discards the file when it answers. Nothing goes to Anthropic and nothing is stored. The Cover Letter tool receives your abstract and the journal name, not the manuscript.
ModernTex
A Mac app. Your documents stay on your Mac, and there is no account. The license key is checked offline against a public key built into the app. Unlocking makes no server request. Once a day the app asks purplelink.llc for its update feed. That request carries the app's version and a channel token compiled into the app, and nothing from your documents. We count update downloads as totals per file. The expired-trial screen opens pages in your browser only when you click. The Buy link carries the trial day and utm tags. The optional reason and trial-download questions are stored as weekly counts with no identifier. The IP address is used only as a salted daily hash for a rate limit and is never stored. If you turn on ModernTex's optional AI features, they use your own AI account, not ours.
Vitae
A Mac app with a local database and no account. Purplelink cannot see your records. Once a day it checks purplelink.llc for a newer version and, unless you hide it, fetches the text of a small sidebar card. Neither request carries an account, device identifier, cookie or library data. Citation lookups go to CrossRef, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, ORCID or Zotero only when you use those features, and your mailbox is read only if you connect one.
Legroom
A Mac app with no account and no analytics. It reads folder and file names, sizes and dates on your Mac to measure space and build previews, and does not send them anywhere. Rules and the Uninstaller show you a preview and wait for your approval. The Uninstaller moves items to the Trash and never deletes them. No permission is required. Full Disk Access is optional, and only if you choose to give it, so Legroom can read folders such as Desktop, Documents and Downloads. The license key is checked on your Mac and sends nothing. Once a day the app asks purplelink.llc for its update feed. That request carries the app's name and version and an update token, with what any web request carries, such as your IP address, and nothing about your files.
Keyfeel
A Mac app with no account and no analytics. It needs the macOS Input Monitoring permission to be told that a key was pressed. It reads the key code and not the character, it does not send key codes or anything else you type, and macOS does not deliver keystrokes from password fields to it. Two optional features, scroll sounds at the end of a page and sounds for Mission Control, Application windows and Show Desktop, also ask for the Accessibility permission. The license key is checked on your Mac and sends nothing. Its only network use is the daily update feed request, which carries the app's name and version and an update token. Settings can list the network connections Keyfeel has open at that moment, and shows the commands to check its signature yourself.
Tapefolio
A Mac app with no account and no analytics. Transcription, speaker labels, finding and replacing identifiers and reading scanned pages run on your Mac, and your recordings, transcripts, images and key files are not uploaded. macOS asks for microphone access the first time you record, and the app records only when you press Record. Apple's on-device speech recognition is used only if you choose it in Settings, and macOS then asks for the Speech Recognition permission. Speaker memory, if you turn it on, is stored encrypted on your Mac. The license key is checked on your Mac and sends nothing. The network is used for the daily update feed request, and for the optional model downloads you start in Settings, which come from Hugging Face. Neither carries anything you opened in the app.
Panely
A Mac app with no account and no analytics. It uses the macOS Accessibility permission to move and resize windows, and reads each window's position, size and title to do so, but not what is inside a window. It does not use Input Monitoring or Screen Recording. Layouts and settings stay on your Mac. The license key is checked on your Mac and sends nothing. Its only network use is the daily update feed request, which carries the app's name and version and an update token. An optional setting, Drag statistics, is off until you turn it on. It writes the distance, speed and timing of drags near a screen edge to a file on your Mac, with no app names or window titles, and Panely does not send it anywhere.
Summonly
A Mac app with no account and no analytics. It indexes the folders you choose, and macOS asks your permission before it reads Desktop, Documents, Downloads or a removable or network volume. It does not need Full Disk Access. The index, and the document text read for question search if you turn that on, stay in Summonly's folder in Application Support on your Mac. Clipboard history is off until you turn it on, skips password managers and items an app marks as private, and is stored in a file only you can read, or in memory only. The license key is checked on your Mac and sends nothing. The network is used for the daily update feed request, for a web search or assistant row, which opens the page in your browser only when you press Return, and for the optional search model: Settings asks purplelink.llc for a small signed description of it, and the model, about 76 MB, downloads only after you click Download and is checked against its signature and checksum before it is installed. None of the requests the app makes itself carries file names, file contents, your searches or clipboard text.
Outbound Veil
A Mac app with no analytics. It reads the text field you are typing in through the macOS Accessibility permission, checks it on your Mac, and does not store it or send it anywhere. Input Monitoring is requested only if you turn on send hold, so the app can notice the send key. The license key is checked on your Mac and sends nothing. Apart from the download, its only network use is the check for a new version, which every copy, trial or licensed, makes.
BatteryMeter
A free Mac menu-bar app with no account, no license and no analytics. It reads the battery entry in macOS and runs the system's own top and ps tools to list the busiest processes while its menu is open. Process names are shown on screen and are not stored or sent. Its menu can quit or force quit a process you own, with a confirmation for force quit and a refusal for system processes. It has no helper tool and does not ask for administrator rights. It declares no entitlements and asks for no macOS permission. It is distributed as a zip with its SHA-256 on the product page. Once a day it fetches the update feed at purplelink.llc/batterymeter/appcast.xml, and it installs an update only after you agree. The Check for updates automatically menu item turns the daily fetch off.
Mac app downloads
The Mac apps are signed with a Developer ID certificate, run with the hardened runtime and are notarized by Apple, so macOS Gatekeeper can check them before they open. Vitae runs in the macOS App Sandbox. ModernTex, Outbound Veil, Legroom, Keyfeel, Tapefolio, Panely, Summonly and BatteryMeter do not,, and the permissions each one asks for are listed above. ModernTex, Outbound Veil, Legroom, Keyfeel, Tapefolio, Panely, Summonly and BatteryMeter update through Sparkle, which checks an EdDSA signature on each update. BatteryMeter also lists the SHA-256 of its download on its page, so you can check the file yourself. What each app sends over the network is in the privacy policy.
Scholar Utility Belt
A Chrome extension that runs on Google Scholar pages. It has no Purplelink server and no analytics. Saved papers, notes and settings stay in the extension's storage in your browser. Lookups you turn on go straight from your browser to public scholarly services such as OpenAlex, CrossRef, Unpaywall and Semantic Scholar. The optional Pro tier is licensed through ExtensionPay.
Payments
Stripe takes payment on its own checkout page. Purplelink never sees card details and creates no account for you.

Encryption

In transit and at rest

In transit
Every page, upload and download on purplelink.llc is served over HTTPS by Netlify, with HTTP Strict Transport Security set for one year, including subdomains. The backend on Modal is reached only over HTTPS.
At rest on Modal
Modal states that all user data on its platform is encrypted in transit and at rest. See Modal's security and privacy page.
At rest on Netlify
Netlify states that it encrypts data at rest and in transit within its infrastructure. See Netlify's security page.

Data classes

What to upload, and what not to

Suitable for
Unpublished manuscripts, preprints, drafts, theses.
Not suitable for
Identifiable patient or participant data, data under a data use agreement, export-controlled material. Purplelink is not a HIPAA business associate. De-identify before uploading.

Incidents and continuity

If something goes wrong

Breach notice
If a security incident affects your data, you will be told by email at the address on your receipt as soon as the facts are known.
Who runs it
Purplelink is one person, Benjamin Ampel. If he is unavailable, a review in progress still finishes. The pipeline runs on its own, and a scheduled job deletes the manuscript and result on the same 30-minute and 24-hour clock. Refunds are handled through Stripe, and the 14-day refund window on paid tools applies.

Report a problem

Reporting a security problem

How to report
Email ben@purplelink.llc with what you found, the page or product it affects, and the steps to reproduce it. Reports for the website and the public source repositories can also be sent privately through GitHub: use "Report a vulnerability" on the Security tab of the purplelink repository. The machine-readable version is at /.well-known/security.txt.
What to expect
Purplelink is one person. Expect a reply within a few days, and a fix or a clear answer as soon as the problem is understood. Please give a reasonable time to fix it before you publish details.
Please do not
Access, change or delete other people's data, run denial-of-service tests, or send spam through the site's forms. Test with your own files and your own purchase. There is no paid bounty program, and a report made in good faith under these rules will not be answered with legal action.
