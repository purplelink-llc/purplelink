Outbound Veil compatibility

Where Outbound Veil works: apps and fields tested

By Benjamin Ampel · October 8, 2026 · results recorded October 3, 2026 on macOS 26

Outbound Veil can only check text that an app hands to macOS. This page lists each app and field it has been tried in, what happened, and which ones have not been tried at all.

Outbound Veil has been tested and works in TextEdit, Notes, Messages, Microsoft Word, and in Chrome text areas, including the ChatGPT and claude.ai prompt boxes. It cannot read the body of a new message in Apple Mail, and it is switched off in Terminal by default. Safari, Slack, VS Code, Pages, Google Docs and most other apps have not been tested.

What the three statuses mean

  • Tested and works. A made-up note was typed or pasted into the field. The badge appeared, and Redact all rewrote the field.
  • Tested and does not work. The field was tried, and Outbound Veil could not read it or could not use it safely.
  • Not tested. No result is recorded. It may work or it may not, and this page does not guess. Every app or field not named below belongs in this group.

Every status is written out in words. The same test text was used each time: Maria Gonzalez, SSN 472-81-0094, born on March 3, 1990. All of it is invented.

The only recorded test run was on one Mac running macOS 26 on October 3, 2026, with builds made before release 1.0.0. Releases 1.0.1, 1.0.2 and 1.1.0 changed power use, the update key and licensing. The tests have not been recorded again since.

Send hold

Send hold is optional and is turned on one app at a time in Settings, on the Apps tab. It holds the send key while a field still has findings. It needs Input Monitoring, which macOS asks for only when you turn a hold on.

What decides whether a field can be read

Outbound Veil reads a field only when macOS describes it as a text field, text area, combo box or search field and hands over its text. Three things follow.

  • An app that draws its own text, or puts a web view where a text field would be, may give macOS nothing to read. Apple Mail is the case that was tested.
  • Password fields, password managers and Outbound Veil’s own windows are skipped. Fields longer than 200,000 characters are skipped.
  • A field that can be read may still refuse a direct edit. Redact all then pastes the replacement text. If that fails too, it puts the redacted text on the clipboard and says so.

Check your own app in the 7-day trial

The trial is the complete app for seven days, with no account. That is enough time to try the apps you use. Use made-up details only.

  1. Download the trial from the Outbound Veil page, open it and allow Accessibility. The setup guide shows where.
  2. Click into a text field in the app you want to test and type or paste Maria Gonzalez, SSN 472-81-0094, born on March 3, 1990. Values such as 555 phone numbers or example.com addresses may not be flagged, so use this line.
  3. Wait a second. A badge with a number of findings near the field means the field can be read. If the menu bar menu says Cannot read this field followed by the app’s name, it cannot. If nothing shows, check that checking is not paused and that Accessibility is still on.
  4. Click the badge and choose Redact all. The field should now hold placeholders such as [SSN_1]. If the app refuses the edit, the redacted text is put on your clipboard with a notice.
  5. Choose Copy details of the focused field in the menu bar menu. It copies the app, the kind of field and whether it is readable and editable, and never what you typed. Email it to ben@purplelink.llc if you want a row added here.
  6. For send hold, open Settings, choose the Apps tab and set the app to Check and hold send. Test it only where a stray send does no harm, such as a document, or a new message with no recipient.

If your app cannot be read, the free trial is how to find out. If you buy and change your mind, email ben@purplelink.llc within 14 days of buying for a refund (terms).

Limits

Latin-script names only. It also flags public figures’ names. It will miss some things. It lowers the chance of a mistake and is not a compliance product.
