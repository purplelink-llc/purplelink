How it works

You ask for a place

Press a shortcut, choose an action from the menu bar, or drag a window to a screen edge or corner. While you drag, a preview shows where the window will land.

Panely moves the window

It moves and resizes the front window through the macOS Accessibility permission. It reads each window's position, size and title, and nothing that is inside the window.

It remembers

Restore returns a window to its size before the first snap. Saved layouts bring back a whole arrangement, and Panely keeps one automatically for each set of displays you use.

What it does

SnappingHalves, quarters, thirds, two thirds, fourths, three fourths and sixths of the screen. Also maximize, full height, full width, almost maximize, center and a larger center. You can also make a window larger or smaller by a step. Each snap can leave a margin around the screen and a gap between windows, from 0 to 32 points.
ShortcutsMost actions have a default shortcut, such as Control-Option with an arrow key. In Settings, click any shortcut and press the keys you want, including for the sixths, which have none to start with. Panely refuses combinations that macOS keeps for itself or that another action already uses, and warns you when another app holds one. Any shortcut can be reset or turned off. Panely uses macOS's own system for global shortcuts, so it does not need the Input Monitoring permission and does not see other keys you press.
DraggingDrag a window to the left or right edge for a half. Drag it to the top edge to maximize, to the bottom edge for a bottom half, or to a corner for a quarter. A translucent preview shows the result before you let go.
Repeat to resizePress a left or right half shortcut again and the window steps to two thirds, then one third, then back to half. This can be turned off.
Resize togetherWhen Panely has put two windows side by side, dragging the edge between them resizes both, so they never overlap or leave a gap. It works for any windows Panely placed, by shortcut, drag, the fill-the-rest list, a layout or a custom area, including a half beside two quarters. A window is never made narrower than 160 points; the edge stops there. Drag a window away by its title bar to unlink it. This can be turned off.
Fill the restAfter you snap a window, Panely can offer the part of the screen that is left to another window or app. The list shows your open windows, your 10 most used apps and a search box that finds any app on your Mac by name. Choose one with a click, or type and press Return, and it fills that space; if the app is not running, Panely opens it first. Both windows come to the front afterwards. Each entry shows the app icon and name and, for a window, its title. If you allow Screen Recording, open windows also show a picture of themselves, like Mission Control, so two windows of one app are easy to tell apart; the pictures are kept only while the list is on screen and are never saved or sent. Escape closes the list at once. By default it appears after every snap, including from the menu bar and by shortcut. After a drag you can type straight away; after a shortcut or a menu choice the list waits until you click into it, so what you type next still goes to your app. Settings can limit it to dragging and the menu, or turn it off. It offers nothing after a maximize, when nothing is left, and a window placed this way can be restored like any other.
Picture in PicturePicture in Picture video windows are left alone: a shortcut, a drag to an edge or a layout does not move or resize them, and they are not offered in the fill-the-rest list. Panely recognises them by their title and small size. A switch in Settings turns this off if you want them snapped. A pop-out window that is not video, such as a call in a small window, may not be recognised.
DisplaysMove a window to the next or previous display and it keeps its proportions. Panely orders displays left to right.
RestoreReturn a window to the size and place it had before its first snap.
Custom areasDefine your own places, up to twelve, such as a reading column in the middle 60% of the screen. Set the left edge, top, width and height as percentages in Settings. Or place a window where you want it and choose Save this window as an area from the menu bar. Each area can have its own shortcut and appears in the menu bar. Areas are fractions of the screen, so they fit any display.
Ignored appsChoose apps whose windows are never moved, by shortcut or by dragging, such as a game or a video call. Open the menu bar icon while that app is in front and choose Never move windows of it.
LayoutsSave the arrangement of your open windows under a name, and apply it from the menu bar. Windows are matched by app, and by title when an app has several windows. Apps with no open window are listed instead of guessed at.
Describe a layoutType something like Xcode left, Simulator right, Terminal bottom. Panely reads the words with fixed rules on your Mac. It asks you when an app name matches more than one app, and it tells you which parts it could not read.
MonitorsPanely keeps an automatic layout for each arrangement of displays. When you plug a display back in, it waits a few seconds for macOS to settle and then puts windows where they were.
Windows it can reachPanely works on the windows you can see in the current Space. macOS gives apps no way to move windows between Spaces, so it does not.

Privacy

Works offline

Panely needs no network connection for any of its features. There are no accounts. The only network use is its check for a new version, which carries the app's name and version.

Positions, sizes and titles

To move a window, Panely reads where it is, how big it is and its title, so a saved layout can tell two windows of one app apart. The list offered after a snap shows app names and window titles too, and reads the use counts macOS already keeps for apps to find the ones you use most. Panely reads what is inside a window only if you turn on window pictures and allow Screen Recording: it then takes one still picture of each open window when the list appears, keeps it in memory while the list is on screen and discards it when the list closes. Nothing is saved or sent. Without that permission it reads nothing from inside a window.

No keystroke access

Shortcuts use macOS's own global shortcut system. Panely does not need Input Monitoring and cannot see which other keys you press. The one short-lived addition is Escape, which Panely registers as a shortcut only while the picker is open, so it can close it at once.

Details are on the privacy page.

The Accessibility permission

When Panely first opens, macOS asks you to allow it under System Settings, Privacy & Security, Accessibility. That is the permission an app needs to move other apps' windows. Without it Panely can still be opened, but it cannot move anything. You can switch it off again in the same place.

What it is not

Automatic tilingIt does not rearrange windows by itself. It acts when you ask: a shortcut, the menu, a drag, or a layout you apply.
An AI appIt does not use a language model. Describing a layout works from a small set of fixed rules, and says when it cannot read a phrase.
A window recorderIt reads window positions, sizes and titles. With Screen Recording allowed, it takes pictures of open windows for the fill-the-rest list only, keeps them while the list is on screen, and never stores or sends them. Saved layouts keep window titles on your Mac, and a title can contain a document name or a web address.
Cross-platformMac only. There is no Windows, iPhone or iPad version.

Frequently asked questions

What does it need to run?
macOS 14 (Sonoma) or later, and the macOS Accessibility permission.
macOS already snaps windows when I drag them. Will they fight?
They can. If you leave both on, a drag to an edge may be snapped twice. Settings tells you when macOS's own edge tiling is on. It has a button that opens the right System Settings pane, where you turn off Drag windows to screen edges to tile.
Does it use AI?
No. Describing a layout in words is done by fixed rules on your Mac, with no model and no network. If a phrase is unclear it asks or says so. It does not guess.
Can it see what is in my windows?
No. It reads each window's position, size and title, which it needs to move and match windows.
Will Panely resize my Picture in Picture window?
No, not by default. A Picture in Picture video window keeps its size and place when you use a shortcut, drag it to an edge or apply a layout, and it is not offered in the fill-the-rest list. Panely recognises it by its title and its small size. A switch in Settings turns this off if you want those windows snapped like any other. A pop-out window that is not video, such as a call in a small window, may not be recognised.
What is the list of windows that appears after I snap one?
It offers the part of the screen your window did not take to another window or app. You see your open windows and your 10 most used apps, and you can type to find any app on your Mac. Choose one with a click, or type and press Return, and Panely puts its window in that space, opening the app first if it is not running. Open windows can show a picture of themselves, the way Mission Control does, once you allow Screen Recording in Settings; without it the list shows app icons and window names. Pictures are kept only while the list is on screen and are never saved or sent. Escape closes it at once. By default it appears after every snap, including from the menu bar and by shortcut; after a shortcut or a menu choice it waits until you click into it, so your typing still goes to your app. Settings can limit it to dragging and the menu, or turn it off.
Will it put windows back when I reconnect a monitor?
It is built to. Panely remembers where windows were for each arrangement of displays and, a few seconds after the arrangement changes back, puts the windows where they were. It only places windows that are open, and only those in the current Space.
Does it work with Spaces and full-screen apps?
It moves windows in the Space you are looking at. It does not move windows between Spaces, and macOS full-screen windows are managed by macOS.
Can I change the shortcuts?
Yes. In Settings, click a shortcut and press the keys you want. Panely refuses combinations that macOS keeps for itself or that another action already uses. Any shortcut can be reset or turned off.
Does one license work on more than one Mac?
One license key works on two of your Macs. Paste the same key into Panely on each one.
Are updates included?
Yes, all of them. The price is a single payment, for life. Panely checks for a new version and offers to install it in place.
What happens when the trial ends?
The trial is the complete app for seven days from the first time you open it, with no account. To keep using Panely after that, buy it. You get a license key by email and on the page after you pay. Choose Enter License Key from the menu bar and paste it. It works offline.
Can I get a refund?
Email ben@purplelink.llc within 14 days of purchase. See the terms.
How do I get help?
Email ben@purplelink.llc. If you lose your download link, the Find a purchase page emails it again to the address you paid with.
Why not Rectangle or Magnet?
Both are good, and Rectangle is free. If you only snap windows to halves, quarters and thirds, you may not need Panely. Panely adds your own areas, up to twelve, and saved layouts that come back when you reconnect a monitor. It costs $9.99 once after a 7-day trial. Magnet is $4.99 on the Mac App Store, and Rectangle Pro has a 10-day trial.
Does it work offline, and does it need an account?
Yes, it works offline, and there is no account. Layouts, shortcuts and settings are kept on your Mac. Pasting a license key works offline. The only network use is the check for a new version.
