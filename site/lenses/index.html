Why an app, when System Settings has a Displays pane

Three things System Settings cannot do for these glasses

It has no edge margin, so the soft edges of the lenses always show picture. Night Shift does not reach the glasses. And nothing applies your settings when the glasses connect or puts them back when you unplug. The other settings, such as the sharp mode and Dark Mode, you can set by hand, and the guide below shows how.

What it does

Sharp modeA HiDPI mode, where text is drawn at 2x and scaled down, and 120 Hz where the glasses offer it. If a profile’s exact size is not offered by the glasses, Lenses picks the nearest HiDPI size.
LayoutMirror the laptop, put the glasses above the laptop, or place them side by side.
Edge marginShrinks the picture to 95%, 90% or 85%, or leaves it off, so the soft edges of the lenses show black with the pixels off. This is underscan, which System Settings does not offer for these glasses.
Night tintOff, Warm, Night or Candle, with an automatic setting from 9 PM to 7 AM. Whenever macOS Night Shift is on, the glasses get a matching warm tint, because Night Shift itself does not reach them.
Dark Mode and pointerWhile the Mirror 70% profile is on, Lenses turns on Dark Mode and a bigger pointer, then puts both back afterwards.
See-Through ModeA switch in the menu. Black is clear on these glasses, so Lenses turns on Dark Mode, makes the desktop black and keeps an edge margin of at least 10%. Only your windows light up, and you see the room around them. Your desktop picture comes back when you unplug.
RestoreOn unplug, and when you quit, the Mac goes back to how it was.
SoundOptionally plays sound through the glasses.
Open at loginA switch in its settings.

Seven profiles

  • Optimal. 1920 by 1080 HiDPI, glasses above the laptop, 120 Hz.
  • Mirror. 1920 by 1080 HiDPI, 60 Hz.
  • Mirror Big Text. 1600 by 900 HiDPI, 120 Hz.
  • Big Text. 1600 by 900 HiDPI, glasses above the laptop.
  • Side by Side. The glasses placed beside the laptop screen.
  • Cinema. Native 1080p at 120 Hz, with the laptop screen dark.
  • Mirror 70%. The picture at 70%, with Dark Mode and a bigger pointer.

Frequently asked questions

What glasses does it work with?
Tested on RayNeo Air. It also recognises XREAL and VITURE glasses by their display name, but I have not tested it on them. For any other glasses, choose Use this display as my glasses from the menu.
What does it need to run?
macOS 14 Sonoma or later, on an Apple silicon Mac.
Why is it not in the Mac App Store?
It uses private macOS display calls, which the Mac App Store does not allow. It is a notarized direct download instead.
What does the edge margin do?
It shrinks the picture to 95%, 90% or 85% of the screen so the soft, blurry edges of the lenses show black. Black pixels are switched off, so nothing is lit there. This is underscan, and System Settings does not offer it for these glasses.
Does Night Shift work on my glasses?
Night Shift does not reach them. Lenses gives the glasses a matching warm tint whenever macOS Night Shift is on. You can also pick Warm, Night or Candle yourself, or let it switch on from 9 PM to 7 AM.
What happens when I unplug the glasses?
Lenses puts the Mac back: the display mode, layout, Dark Mode and pointer size you had before. It does the same when you quit it.
Does it collect anything?
No. Lenses collects nothing. The only network request it makes is the update check, which sends no information about your displays or settings. Its settings stay on your Mac.
Is there a trial, and what happens when it ends?
Yes. The same download runs free for 7 days from the first time you open it, with no account. After that, Lenses stops applying profiles until you enter a license key, which costs $4.99 once and arrives by email and on the page after you pay. If it does not suit your glasses, you have 14 days to ask for a full refund.
Can I get a refund?
Yes. Email ben@purplelink.llc within 14 days, no reason needed, for the full $4.99. A refunded purchase's download link stops working. See the terms.
Is it part of the Mac Suite?
Yes. The Mac Suite includes Lenses at the same $59.99, and anyone who already bought the Suite gets it too. You can also buy Lenses alone for $4.99.
