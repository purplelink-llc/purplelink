Sent to the wrong person: what Apple Mail can and cannot catch

By Benjamin Ampel · October 14, 2026

Apple Mail on a Mac can hold a message for a few seconds after you press Send, and it can send one later. Neither checks who the message is going to or what is attached. This guide says what each does, offers a short habit list, and says where my own app does not help.

Disclosure: Purplelink, which publishes this site, makes Outbound Veil, a Mac menu-bar app that checks typed text for personal details. It is mentioned near the end, after the part that does not depend on it, and I say plainly what it cannot do with email. I read the pages cited below on October 14, 2026. I did not test Apple Mail's settings for this guide, so the Apple settings are described as the pages describe them.

The short answer

Turn on a longer Undo Send delay in Mail, read the To line before the body, and check the attachment name before you press Send. Apple Mail has no built-in check on the recipient. Outbound Veil does not check recipients or attachments either; it checks only the text you type.

Three ways it goes wrong

A legal technology site, Legal IT Insider, described email sent to the wrong recipient as "a really common problem" and a particular challenge for organisations that routinely deal with highly sensitive information, law firms among them (Legal IT Insider, February 16, 2018). That page is a short promotion for a vendor webinar. It gives no figures, and it does not discuss attachments, Bcc or address suggestions, so I do not rely on it for how often any of this happens.

These are three ways it can go wrong. I have no source on how common each one is.

  • The wrong recipient. A suggested address is accepted without a second look, or Reply All goes to people who should not see the answer.
  • The wrong attachment. The message is right and the file is the draft, or another client's.
  • Bcc exposed. A group goes in To or Cc, and every recipient sees every address.

What Apple Mail gives you

As described by Apple on October 14, 2026, in the Mail User Guide for Mac, and as described by MacRumors in an article dated November 2, 2022:

If you want more time, MacRumors describes setting the delay to 30 seconds. That is a setting, not a check. It helps only if you look at the message again during the window.

Habits to try

This is plain advice. I am not claiming any of it prevents a mistake.

  1. Read the To line before the body. Do it last, with the cursor away from the Send button.
  2. Read the attachment name. Open it if the name is generic.
  3. Use Bcc for groups. Put your own address in To.
  4. Retype a client's address instead of accepting a suggestion, at least for sensitive messages.
  5. Be slow on Reply All. Look at who is on the thread first.

Where Outbound Veil fits

Outbound Veil checks typed text for personal details such as names, phone numbers, card numbers and Social Security numbers. Its pages describe nothing that checks recipients or attachments, so do not count on it for either. Its page says names are recognized in Latin script only, that it will miss some things, and that it can flag things that are not personal information.

Since version 1.3.0 (October 24, 2026) it reads the body of a new message in Apple Mail and highlights personal details as you type. It does not offer Redact all there, because rewriting the message would lose its formatting and attachments, so you edit the highlighted details by hand. The table of tested apps and fields also lists TextEdit, Notes, Messages, Microsoft Word and Chrome text areas, including the ChatGPT and claude.ai prompt boxes. Safari, Slack and most other apps have not been tested.

The honest use for email is narrow. You could draft a note in one of those apps, check it there, and paste it into Mail. That adds a step, and it does nothing about the To line or the attachment. The app is on its own page, with a free 7-day trial, and its page says it is not a compliance product.

What the rest of the job needs

This is not compliance advice. A small practice that sends client email still has questions that Mail on a Mac does not answer. Ask the provider that hosts your email whether it offers rules applied on the server, which apply whichever app sends the message, and whether it offers an encrypted-send option. Write down a short policy on who may email what to whom, and who checks. I have not read any provider's documentation for this guide, so I make no claim about what any of them offer.

Frequently asked

Can I unsend an email in Apple Mail on a Mac?

Within a short window, yes. Apple's Mail guide says to click Undo Send at the bottom of the sidebar within 10 seconds of sending, or choose Edit, Undo. You can change the delay or turn it off in Mail, Settings, Composing. MacRumors lists the delay options as Off, 10, 20 and 30 seconds and says the feature needs macOS 13 or later. After the window closes, the message has been sent.

Does Apple Mail warn me if the address looks wrong?

Nothing I read on Apple's Mail pages describes a warning about who a message is addressed to. Undo Send and Send Later change when a message goes, not who it goes to.

Does Outbound Veil check the recipient or the attachment?

No. It checks the text of the field you are typing in for personal details. Its pages describe nothing that checks recipients or attachments. Since version 1.3.0 it does read the body of a new message in Apple Mail.

Does Outbound Veil work in Apple Mail?

Yes, since version 1.3.0, for the body of a new message. It highlights personal details as you type; you edit them by hand, because it never rewrites a Mail message. It also works in TextEdit, Notes, Messages, Microsoft Word and Chrome text areas.

Is this compliance advice?

No. It is a description of what two tools do. A practice with legal duties around client email needs advice about those duties and a written policy.
