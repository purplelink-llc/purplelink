Do researchers need a VPN? University VPNs, public Wi-Fi and travel (2026)

By Benjamin Ampel · October 14, 2026

Most researchers do not need to pay for a VPN. Your university VPN gets you into campus systems, not privacy, and most web traffic is already encrypted. A commercial VPN earns its place on networks you do not trust and when you travel somewhere that blocks the services you work with.

The short answer

In the week of October 11, 2026, at least 96% of pages loaded in Chrome on a Mac used HTTPS (Google Transparency Report). HTTPS encrypts what you send and receive, so a coffee-shop network cannot read your password or your data. The FTC now says that using public Wi-Fi is usually safe (FTC). The EFF makes the same point, and adds that a VPN is not a tool for anonymity, because the VPN provider can see the traffic it carries (EFF Surveillance Self-Defense). A VPN still helps in two situations: hiding which sites you visit from a network you do not trust, and reaching services that the country you are in blocks.

Your university VPN is a different tool

A university VPN connects your laptop to the campus network. It exists so you can reach university systems and library databases from home. It is not a privacy tool. Your traffic goes to the university, which runs the VPN and can see what passes through it.

  • Split tunnel versus full tunnel. Many campus VPNs send only campus traffic through the VPN by default. The University of Illinois, for example, says its default sends only university traffic to campus, and that library resources need the "Tunnel All" setting (Illinois VPN profiles).
  • Library access may not use the VPN at all. NC State's libraries say journal sites bypass the campus VPN, and that you reach databases through the library website instead (NC State University Libraries).
  • Use it when you travel. It is often the most reliable way to reach your university email and files from abroad (see the travel section below).

When a commercial VPN helps

  • A network you do not trust. On hotel, conference or airport Wi-Fi, HTTPS protects the content, but the network can still see which sites you visit. A VPN hides that from the network and shows it to the VPN provider instead.
  • Travel to a country that blocks your tools. Imperial College London's guidance says many apps and websites are blocked in mainland China, including productivity, search and messaging services (Imperial College London). Check your university's own guidance first.
  • Sensitive fieldwork. If your research puts you or participants at risk from a local network operator, talk to your university's information security office. A consumer VPN is one part of that plan, not the plan.

What a VPN does not do

  • It does not stop phishing. A fake login page works the same with or without a VPN. Two-factor sign-in and a password manager matter more.
  • It does not make you anonymous. Logged-in accounts, cookies and browser fingerprinting still identify you, as the EFF guide explains.
  • It does not protect a compromised laptop. Keep macOS updated and turn on FileVault, which encrypts your disk if the laptop is lost or stolen.

Before you travel

  1. Install your university VPN on every device before you leave. The University of Minnesota tells travellers to China to set it up before departure, because downloading it there may not be possible (University of Minnesota).
  2. Ask your IT and export-control offices. Some universities lend a clean laptop for certain destinations, and some research data or software needs an export license.
  3. Set up email forwarding or a second way to reach collaborators, in case your usual service is blocked.
  4. Encrypt the laptop. FileVault on a Mac protects data if the device is lost.

Frequently asked

Is my university VPN private?

No. It sends your traffic to the university network, so the university can see it. It exists to give you campus and library access from elsewhere.

Do I need a VPN on conference or hotel Wi-Fi?

Usually not for safety. Most sites use HTTPS, which encrypts what you send. A VPN also hides which sites you visit from whoever runs the network. If that matters to you, a free option is enough for occasional use.

Will a VPN protect me from phishing?

No. A VPN changes the route your traffic takes. It does not stop you from typing your password into a fake login page. Two-factor sign-in and a password manager do far more against phishing.

Can I use a VPN in China?

Universities that send researchers to China tell them to set up the university VPN before they leave, because downloading it there may not be possible. Ask your IT office and export-control office before you travel, since some universities lend a separate laptop.

Is a free VPN safe?

Some are, some are not. A VPN provider can see the traffic it carries, so choose one with a published no-logs audit. Proton VPN's free plan has no data limit and its apps are open source.
