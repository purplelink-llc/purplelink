BatteryMeter

BatteryMeter puts your MacBook's charge, the direction it is moving and the battery's power in watts in the menu bar. It is free. There is no email to give, no account, no trial and no license key.

See what is using the power

Version 1.1.0 adds a section to the menu called Using the most power. It lists the top five apps and background processes by energy impact. Energy impact is a relative score, not watts. It works like the Energy Impact column in Activity Monitor: a higher number means more power use than a lower one in the same list.

Processes are grouped by app, so a browser's helper processes add up under the browser. A command-line tool inside a large app bundle also groups under that app. The list is measured when you open the menu. It takes about two seconds and shows Measuring first. The menu has a Show top consumers toggle that turns the section off. From version 1.4.0 an optional background watch, described below, also checks once a minute, on battery only.

Each row has a submenu with Quit and Force Quit. The app limits what it will quit:

  • Your own processes only. It does not offer to quit anything that belongs to another user.
  • System processes are protected. kernel_task, launchd, WindowServer, loginwindow, Finder, Dock, SystemUIServer, ControlCenter, coreaudiod and anything under /System or /usr/libexec show no quit options. Neither does BatteryMeter itself.
  • Force Quit asks first. A confirmation appears, and Cancel is the default.
  • It checks before it signals. It re-checks that the process is still the same program before it sends anything.
  • It tells you what happened. After Quit it waits a few seconds and says whether the app quit. If the app is still open, perhaps waiting for you to save, it brings the app forward and offers Force Quit.
  • Several at once. Quit Apps to Save Battery opens a window with a checkbox for each app. Nothing is ticked until you tick it.
  • No helper tool. It installs no helper and stores no password. Low Power Mode is the one setting that needs administrator rights, so macOS shows its own password or Touch ID prompt each time you change it.

Some background items cannot be quit from the menu, and the menu now says why for each one. For those, use Activity Monitor. The list runs /usr/bin/top and /bin/ps on your Mac. Process names are shown on screen and never stored, and the app does not send them anywhere.

Save battery

Version 1.4.0 adds ways to stretch a charge. None of them quits an app on its own.

  • Low Power Mode. Turn on macOS Low Power Mode from the menu, on battery only or always. macOS asks for your password or Touch ID each time, because the setting needs administrator rights.
  • Max Battery. One switch turns on Low Power Mode, dims the built-in display to a level you choose (40 percent unless you change it) and, if you tick it, turns Wi-Fi off. Turning it off or plugging in puts the brightness and Wi-Fi back. Putting Low Power Mode back needs your password, so the menu offers it as one click.
  • Background watch. On battery, once a minute, it checks which of your apps are busy. It tells you when an app has stayed busy for five minutes, when an app has kept an idle Mac awake for 20 minutes, or when the Mac is running hot, and it says why. It checks nothing on power, and each check uses about a hundredth of a second of processor time.
  • Efficiency cores. For apps you choose, it runs them on the Mac's low-power cores while they are in the background on battery, and puts them back when you switch to them or plug in.
  • It learns from your answers. It stops suggesting what you keep dismissing. Only the efficiency-core move can become automatic, after you accept it three times for an app and turn it on, and every automatic move comes with Undo.

What it does

Menu barA battery icon, the percent, an arrow and the battery's power in watts (W, the rate of energy flow). The arrow points up while the charge rises, down while it falls and shows a dot when it is steady. Power has a plus sign while charging and a minus sign while draining.
ColorsGreen while charging. Orange at 20 percent or below on battery. Red at 10 percent or below. The icon fill animates for a few cycles when charging starts or the charge crosses a level, then holds a still charging icon. It follows the macOS Reduce Motion setting and can be turned off.
Menu bar textChoose percent only, percent and power, percent and time, or all three.
The menuStatus and adapter. Charge in mAh (milliamp-hours). Time to full or empty. Battery power with volts and amps. The rate in percent per hour. System use in watts. Watts, volts and amps from the adapter, and the adapter rating. Capacity against design (raw), and the nominal charge capacity. Cycle count. A chart of the charge over the last hour. A Using the most power section, described above.
Health figuresApple's own health figure uses its own method and can differ from the capacity BatteryMeter shows. The menu says so beside the numbers.
Menu itemsCopy details, Show top consumers (on by default, untick it to turn the power list off), Open at login (off until you tick it), Battery settings, Check for updates and Quit.
RefreshThe battery readings refresh when macOS reports a power change, such as plugging in or unplugging the charger, and on a slow timer in between. The power list is measured when you open the menu. The background watch, if it is on, checks once a minute on battery and not at all on power.
UpdatesFrom version 1.2.0 it checks for a newer version once a day and when you choose Check for updates in the menu. The Check for updates automatically menu item turns the daily check off. It asks before it installs. Updates are signed, and the app installs one only if the signature checks out.

Put it where Apple's icon was

macOS keeps its own battery icon in the menu bar. Hide that one and move BatteryMeter into its place.

  1. Hide Apple's icon. Open System Settings, then Control Center, then Battery. Turn off Show in Menu Bar.
  2. Hold Command. Keep the Command key down.
  3. Drag it into place. With Command held, drag BatteryMeter's item to the spot where Apple's icon was. macOS remembers the position.

Privacy

One source, read locally

BatteryMeter reads one entry in the macOS I/O registry (the list of hardware and its readings), the AppleSmartBattery entry. The Terminal command ioreg -rn AppleSmartBattery reads the same source. For the power list it runs /usr/bin/top and /bin/ps on your Mac when you open the menu.

One daily update check

Its only network request is a check once a day of purplelink.llc/batterymeter/appcast.xml for a newer version. The request carries no account, no battery data and no list of processes. As with any web request, our server sees your IP address and the app's name and version. A menu item, Check for updates automatically, turns the check off. It collects nothing and has no analytics and no account. Process names are shown on screen and never stored.

Nothing changed

It stores only its display preferences on your Mac. It changes no system setting. Quit and Force Quit act only on a process you pick, and only your own.

Details are on the privacy page.

What it does not do

Watts per appEnergy impact is a relative score, not watts. BatteryMeter does not measure how many watts one app uses.
Background measuringOn power it measures nothing in the background. On battery the background watch checks once a minute, and the menu turns it off. No reading or process name is sent anywhere.
Installing updates unaskedIt checks daily, unless you turn that off, and installs only after you agree. A menu item, Check for updates automatically, turns the daily check off. With it off, the app makes no network request until you choose Check for updates.
Quitting everythingIt will not quit system processes or other users' processes, and some background items cannot be quit from the menu. It never quits an app on its own.
TemperatureIt shows a temperature only if macOS provides one. The registry entry on the Mac used for testing has none.
Intel MacsIt is built for Apple silicon only.
Desktop MacsA Mac without a battery shows No battery.
A time estimate of its ownThe time to full or empty is macOS's own estimate. BatteryMeter does not compute a different one.
The Mac App StoreIt is not sold there. You download it from this page.

Frequently asked questions

Does it work on a desktop Mac?
No. BatteryMeter reads the battery, so a Mac without one shows No battery. It is for MacBooks.
Does it send anything?
BatteryMeter makes one kind of network request. Once a day it fetches purplelink.llc/batterymeter/appcast.xml to see whether a newer version exists. That request carries no account, no battery data and no list of processes. As with any web request, our server sees your IP address and the app's name and version. BatteryMeter has no analytics, collects nothing and stores only its display preferences on your Mac. A menu item, Check for updates automatically, turns the daily check off. With it off, the app makes no network request until you choose Check for updates.
Why does its capacity differ from the health figure in System Settings?
BatteryMeter shows capacity against design (raw) and the nominal charge capacity, both read from the battery. Apple's own health figure uses its own method and can differ.
Does it show the battery temperature?
Only if macOS provides it. The battery entry on the Mac used for testing has no temperature, so the app shows none there.
Does it run on an Intel Mac?
No. BatteryMeter is built for Apple silicon (M-series chips) only, and it needs macOS 13 or later.
How do I remove it?
Quit BatteryMeter from its menu. If you ticked Open at login, untick it first. Then drag BatteryMeter from Applications to the Trash.
How does it update?
BatteryMeter checks once a day for a newer version, and Check for updates in its menu checks on demand. A menu item, Check for updates automatically, turns the daily check off. With it off, the app makes no network request until you choose Check for updates. It downloads an update only when the update is signed by Purplelink and passes macOS's signature check, and it asks before it installs anything. Versions up to 1.1.0 have no updater, so if you have 1.1.0 or earlier, download the current version from this page once by hand. Updates come through the app from then on. The changes in each version are listed on the changelog.
Can it force quit apps?
Yes, for your own apps and background processes. Hover a row in the Using the most power list and choose Quit or Force Quit. Force Quit asks you to confirm first, and Cancel is the default. System processes such as kernel_task, launchd, WindowServer, loginwindow, Finder and Dock show no quit options, and neither does BatteryMeter itself. Before it sends a signal it checks that the process is still the same program. It uses no sudo and no helper tool.
Does the energy impact number mean watts?
No. Energy impact is a relative score, not watts. It is like the Energy Impact column in Activity Monitor: a higher number means more power use than a lower one in the same list, but the numbers do not convert to watts. The battery power and system use lines in the menu are the figures in watts.
