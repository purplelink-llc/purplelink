Checklist

Before you paste client notes into ChatGPT or Claude: a checklist for Mac users

By Benjamin Ampel · October 11, 2026, updated October 28, 2026

Pasting a note into a chatbot takes a second. These five checks take about two minutes. They cover what ChatGPT and Claude say they do with what you paste, what to remove first, and what to do if you already sent something you should not have.

Disclosure up front: I make Outbound Veil, a Mac menu-bar app that checks what you type for personal information, and it appears in step 4. The comparison of tools, with several free ones, is in a separate guide. I read the provider pages below on October 11, 2026. They change, so check the pages for your plan. This is not legal advice.

The five checks

  1. Know which account you are using. A personal ChatGPT or Claude account and a workspace your employer runs follow different rules.
  2. Check the training setting for that account. It is a toggle, and turning it off does less than people expect.
  3. Remove identifiers before you send, not after. Replace them with placeholders and keep the key somewhere else.
  4. Have something besides your own eyes check the text. Names and numbers hide in free-text notes.
  5. If the data must never leave your machine, do not send it. A local model, or no AI at all, is the answer.

1 and 2: what the providers say about a personal account

ChatGPT. The setting is called Improve the model for everyone, under Settings, then Data controls. OpenAI says that when it is off, your new conversations are not used to train its models, and that turning it off does not delete or hide saved chats. A temporary chat does not appear in your history, does not create or update memories and is not used to improve models. However, OpenAI says it may be retained for up to 30 days for safety purposes. If you give a thumbs up or down, OpenAI says the whole conversation may be used to train its models, even if you opted out. For Business, Enterprise, Edu and ChatGPT for Healthcare workspaces, OpenAI says it does not use content to train its models by default. Source: Data controls in ChatGPT.

Claude. The setting is Help Improve our AI models, under Settings, then Privacy. Anthropic says that when it is off, new chats and coding sessions are not used for future model training, and that data already used for training is not removed. Flagged conversations may still be used to improve its internal trust and safety models. When it is on, Anthropic says it may keep de-identified chats for up to 5 years in its training pipelines, for new or resumed chats. It says Incognito chats are not used to improve Claude even when the setting is on. I could not find the default position of the toggle on those pages, so open it and look. Sources: how to change the setting (updated August 3, 2026) and how long data is stored (July 1, 2026).

One point holds for both: not used for training is not the same as not kept. Each keeps conversations for a while, and a training setting only changes what happens to new ones.

3: remove identifiers first

Check the text for the obvious ones: names, street addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, government, tax, bank and card numbers, dates of birth and record numbers. The de-identification rule of HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act) lists 18 kinds of identifiers in 45 CFR 164.514(b). The list is a good prompt even if you are not covered by HIPAA. Then look for the quiet ones: a rare job in a small town, a named employer, a third party mentioned in passing.

Replace each identifier with a consistent placeholder (Client A, Client B, early March) so the note still reads sensibly. Keep the list of what each placeholder stands for in a file that never goes near the chatbot. Do this before you paste. Editing a chat afterwards does not take the original back.

California lawyers: SB 574 from January 1, 2027

California's SB 574 (Chapter 858 of 2026) was signed on September 30, 2026 and takes effect on January 1, 2027, as Holland & Knight notes. It adds section 6068.1 to the Business and Professions Code. A lawyer who uses generative AI must not enter confidential, personal identifying or other nonpublic information into a system unless access to what the lawyer enters is restricted to the lawyer and people the lawyer has authorized who are bound to keep it confidential. The same section requires reasonable steps to check AI output, including every case and statute citation, and disclosure to the court when AI was used on a document filed there. Source: the chaptered text, read October 21, 2026.

The section lists what counts as personal identifying information: driver's license numbers, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, criminal identification numbers, the addresses and phone numbers of parties, victims, witnesses and court personnel, medical or psychiatric information, financial information, account numbers, and anything sealed by court order or made confidential by court rule or statute. Some of these follow a pattern a checker can spot, such as a Social Security number or a date of birth. Others, such as a psychiatric history told in plain words or a sealed fact, are recognizable only in context. Whether a particular AI service meets the access test depends on its terms and your account, which no redaction tool can tell you. This is not legal advice: read the section and your bar's guidance.

For the daily habit the checks above still apply. Removing a client's name is not enough on its own, because facts, dates and places can identify someone. A tool can help you notice identifiers before you paste; it cannot decide what counts as confidential under the law.

On a Mac: Apple Intelligence and the ChatGPT extension

On a Mac, an AI tool is built into almost every text field, the same fields where you write client notes. Apple's pages for macOS 27, read on October 28, 2026, describe two different paths for that text, and only one of them goes to a third party.

Apple's own models. Apple says many Apple Intelligence requests run entirely on the Mac. Larger ones go to its Private Cloud Compute servers, where it says the data is not stored or made accessible to Apple and is not kept after the answer is returned (Apple Intelligence and privacy on Mac). You can see which requests left the Mac: System Settings, Privacy & Security, Apple Intelligence Report, then choose the last 15 minutes or the last 7 days and click Export Activity. That is Apple's description of its own service; I have not checked it.

The ChatGPT extension. When Apple Intelligence hands a request to ChatGPT, Apple says the request and any attachment go to OpenAI, with your time zone, country, device type, language and the feature you used. Your IP address is hidden, and your general location is sent (Use ChatGPT with Apple Intelligence on Mac). Three settings decide how that happens:

  • Turn it off. System Settings, Siri, ChatGPT, then turn off Use ChatGPT. If your Mac uses Siri AI, the path is System Settings, Siri, Extensions, ChatGPT, then turn off Use Extension. Screen Time can also block the extension.
  • Confirm Requests. Under the ChatGPT extension in Siri settings. With it on, the Mac asks before it sends a request to ChatGPT. Apple says this option is not available with Siri AI, so on those Macs turning the extension off is the setting that matters.
  • Signed in or not. Without a ChatGPT account, Apple says OpenAI does not get information tied to your Apple Account and may use the request only to answer it, not to train its models. If you sign in to a ChatGPT account, your ChatGPT account settings and OpenAI's privacy policy apply instead, and the requests are saved in your chat history.

If client text must not reach a third party, turn the ChatGPT extension off on every Mac where you write notes, and check again after a macOS update. Whether sending client text to Apple's own servers is acceptable is a question for your licensing board or bar, not for a settings page. This is a description of Apple's settings, not legal advice.

4: a check as you type

A tired person misses things in their own notes. A checker that runs where you type catches some of them before you press send. Outbound Veil runs on your Mac, with the checking done locally and nothing you type stored or sent. It marks names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, Social Security, card, passport, driver's license, tax and bank numbers, dates of birth, medical record numbers and IBANs (International Bank Account Numbers). A Redact all command replaces them with placeholders.

What I have tested is narrow. On October 3, 2026, I ran it on one Mac running macOS 26 with builds made before release 1.0.0. It found and redacted a made-up note in the ChatGPT prompt box and in the claude.ai prompt box in Chrome. I have not tested Safari or other browsers, and I have not run those tests again on later releases. The compatibility page records every app and field, including the ones that do not work. Since version 1.3.0 it also reads the body of a new message in Apple Mail, where it highlights details for you to edit by hand. It handles Latin-script names only. It also flags public figures' names, and it will miss some things. It is $29.99 once after a 7-day trial. The free options are in the comparison guide.

5: when the data must not leave your machine

If a policy, a consent form or common sense says the text cannot go to a third party, no setting fixes that. A model that runs on your own Mac keeps the text there, and so does doing the task by hand. The comparison guide covers that option next to the redaction tools.

If you already pasted something you should not have

Delete the chat. OpenAI says it is removed from your view at once and scheduled for permanent deletion within 30 days. The exceptions are data already de-identified or held for security or legal reasons (Deleting and archiving chats). Anthropic says deleted conversations leave your history immediately and its back-end storage within 30 days. Neither undoes training that has already happened. Then tell whoever sets the rules for that data, such as your privacy officer, your IRB or your employer, and follow their process. The deadline for that conversation is theirs, not the provider's.

Frequently asked

If I turn off training, does ChatGPT stop keeping my chat?

No. OpenAI's page says turning off Improve the model for everyone does not delete or hide saved chats. They stay in your history. A temporary chat is the option that stays out of history, and OpenAI says it may be retained for up to 30 days for safety.

Is deleting a chat enough?

It removes it from your view at once. OpenAI says it then schedules the chat for permanent deletion within 30 days, with exceptions for data already de-identified or kept for security or legal reasons. Anthropic says deleted chats are removed from your history immediately and from its back-end storage within 30 days. Deleting does not undo training that has already happened.

Does a redaction tool make this compliant?

No. A checker lowers the chance of a mistake. It does not make a workflow compliant with HIPAA, FERPA (Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act), your IRB (institutional review board) protocol or your employer's policy. Every tool misses some things. Ask whoever sets the rules where you work. If you work for a HIPAA covered entity, HHS says a vendor that handles protected health information for you needs a business associate agreement (BAA) first. OpenAI says it offers one for its API, for sales-managed ChatGPT Enterprise and Edu, and for ChatGPT for Clinicians, and not for ChatGPT Business. Anthropic says it offers one for its API and for HIPAA-ready Claude Enterprise plans once the organization's owner turns it on. Whether you need one is a question for your compliance officer, not for me. (Pages read October 14, 2026.)

Does California's SB 574 apply to me?

From January 1, 2027 its section 6068.1 applies to attorneys in California who use generative AI; other sections of the same law cover arbitrators and the courts. If you are not one of them, your own professional rules and your employer's policy decide what you may paste. Read the enacted text or ask your bar. This is not legal advice. The section above summarizes it.

Does Apple Intelligence send my notes to ChatGPT?

Only through the ChatGPT extension. Apple says a request handed to ChatGPT goes to OpenAI with the text and any attachment. You can turn the extension off in System Settings, Siri. Apple's own models run on the Mac or on its Private Cloud Compute servers, which it says do not store the request. The section above lists the settings.

Which is safer, ChatGPT or Claude?

I did not compare them on safety, and I cannot tell you. Both describe a training control and a 30-day deletion window for personal accounts, and OpenAI says it treats business workspaces differently. I did not verify how Anthropic treats its business plans. Read the pages linked above for the plan you actually use.
