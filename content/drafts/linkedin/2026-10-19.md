## Option 1

I spend most of my day running several Claude Code sessions at once, working on different parts of Purplelink. The problem was always the same: a session would finish, or hit a question it needed me to answer, and I would not notice for ten or fifteen minutes because it was buried behind other windows.

So I built Buddy, a small app that lives in the MacBook notch. It shows what each session is doing, and when one needs you, it makes a sound, opens with an orange badge for twenty seconds, and lets you click straight into that chat, terminal tab, or editor window. When a usage limit pauses your sessions, it counts down to the reset and can type "continue" into them for you.

Buddy makes no network connections. It reads a folder of hook events that only exists while it is running, plus the last 64 KB of each session's transcript, and nothing leaves the Mac. I released it free and open source, not as a Purplelink product with a price, because it solves a problem I hit every day and I would rather other people building with Claude Code have it too.

Version 0.2.1 shipped this weekend with a new icon and notch character.

https://purplelink.llc/buddy/

If you have built a small tool just to fix your own workflow, I would like to hear what it does.

## Option 2

Purplelink's company accounts post once a day, picked from new releases, guides and tools. This week I rewrote how that automated post gets written for Mastodon, because I caught the old version doing something I would not want a person to do.

The early template had a fixed opening line: "I shipped this release this week, and the first changes from the changelog are listed here." It was technically accurate and said nothing at all. It was filler wearing the shape of a sentence.

So I replaced it. Every post now has to lead with the same real fact the other platforms use, the app name and version or the guide title, then quote sentences straight from the product page, and for a release it can close with one more verified fact from that page, such as the macOS version required. I also listed out the old filler phrases by hand and wired the vetting gate to reject any post that still uses them.

The gate already checked every post against the page it describes and rejected claims it could not verify. Teaching it to catch filler, not just falsehoods, felt like the harder and more honest fix, since filler is not wrong, it is just empty.

I would be glad to hear how you draw the line between a vetting rule and a judgment call in your own work.

## Option 3

I added click tracking for affiliate links on purplelink.llc this week, and spent more time deciding what not to collect than what to build.

The site already tracks which free tools people run and which paid offers they click, through a same-origin script with no cookies and no fingerprinting, and it honors Do Not Track. The affiliate links on pages like the research desk needed the same kind of count. The easy version would log the destination URL on every click. I decided against it: the script now records only the program name and the page the click came from, and the full URL never leaves the browser.

It is a small decision that will never show up in a feature list or a changelog anyone reads closely. But it is the same rule I apply across every product: Outbound Veil, Legroom, Summonly, all of them run with no cloud sync, no accounts, and analytics that stop at a count. Running this as a one-person company means there is no one else to catch it if I cut a corner for convenience, so the standard has to hold on its own, every time, with no one checking.

https://purplelink.llc/privacy/

I would like to know what you check before adding any kind of tracking to something you run.

## Selected
Option 1 -- it is the most concrete and relatable of the three: a specific daily problem, a free open-source tool built to fix it, and verifiable facts (no network access, what it reads, this weekend's version). It carries no risk of undermining trust the way explaining an automated-posting system might, and it fits the audience (researchers and Apple-platform people who may themselves use Claude Code) better than an internal analytics decision.
