What GlobePin does that no other travel app does

By Benjamin Ampel · May 27, 2026

Every travel tracker on the App Store solves a piece of the problem. Most miss the same things. This post explains what I built differently and why.

I've tried a lot of travel tracking apps. Most of them fall into one of two categories: the social-network kind that wants you to check in for other people's benefit, and the minimal kind that is basically a spreadsheet. Neither is what I was looking for.

I wanted something in between: a complete personal travel record with a real map, not just a list of countries visited. It would hold every city, every flight, every place I had been, and where I wanted to go next. I also wanted a record I would enjoy looking at.

GlobePin started as that record. As I built it, I kept running into the same gaps in the existing options. The sections below cover what I did differently.

The complete flight record

Most travel apps track destinations, not the trips between them. You mark a country as visited and move on. However, the trips matter. The eleven-hour flight to Tokyo, the connection through Heathrow, and the short hop from Barcelona to Madrid are all part of the travel story.

GlobePin tracks every flight: departure airport, arrival airport, date, distance, duration. That data feeds into stats such as total miles flown, flight count by year, longest route, and most visited airports. For frequent travelers, the record grows more useful over time.

A 3D globe for long-haul routes

Map apps use flat projections. Most travel trackers do too. A flat map distorts how the world looks, and it makes long-haul routes look strange. A direct flight from New York to Tokyo crossing the Pacific is not a straight line on a Mercator projection (the standard flat world map).

GlobePin has a 3D globe view because it shows routes the way they exist on the earth's surface. Watching great circle routes (the shortest path between two points on a sphere) arc across the globe gives context that a flat map cannot. It changes how you see your own travel history.

Travel goals that aren't checklists

Most travel goal features are checklists. You check off countries and collect badges.

GlobePin's goals work differently. You set a goal such as "visit every continent," "fly 50,000 miles," or "spend time in 10 new countries this year," and your existing travel data fills in progress automatically. If you have already been to four continents, the app knows and shows you what is left. You never update progress by hand.

Anniversary reminders

This feature is small, and I have not seen it in other apps. GlobePin can remind you when the anniversary of a trip is coming up ("One year ago today, you arrived in Kyoto."). The reminder makes a data app feel more personal.

Shareable postcards without the social network

Travel apps that have sharing built in usually want you to share inside the app, with followers or friends, as part of their retention loop. I did not want that model.

GlobePin generates shareable travel postcards (a single image combining your map, your stats, and a personal note) that you export and share wherever you want, such as iMessage, Instagram, or email. The app is a tool, not the destination.

iCloud sync, not a proprietary account

Your travel data should be yours. GlobePin syncs across devices using iCloud via CloudKit (Apple's sync infrastructure, which you already pay for as an iPhone owner). There is no account, no subscription for sync, and no data stored on Purplelink's servers.

If GlobePin ever went away, your data would still be in iCloud.

GlobePin 1.0 is on the App Store as of 3 September 2026. If you want a real record of everywhere you have been, GlobePin is free to download.
