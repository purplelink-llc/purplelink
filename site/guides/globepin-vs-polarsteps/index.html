Comparison

GlobePin vs Polarsteps vs Visited

By Benjamin Ampel · May 31, 2026

Three iOS apps for tracking where you've been. They look similar in the App Store but solve different problems. Here's the honest breakdown.

Disclosure: I'm building one of these apps (GlobePin). I've tried to write the comparison honestly. Polarsteps is the right pick if you want narrative travel journaling; Visited is the right pick if you want a quick "places I've been" list. GlobePin is for the third use case: a personal travel record that emphasizes the map and the flight log over the journal.

The three approaches

Polarsteps: the travel journal

Polarsteps is the largest of the three; its site says 22M+ travelers. The core experience is: you go on a trip, the app records your route via background location, you add photos and notes day-by-day, and the result is a sharable trip "story" with a printed photo book as an upsell.

Strengths: automatic route tracking that works offline, a polished story format, and a printed hardback travel book. It is free to download. The optional Plus subscription adds 3D maps, extra map styles and more travel buddies, and is listed at $9.99 a month or $34.99 a year on the US App Store (prices vary by region).

Limitations: tracking needs background-location permission (battery and privacy implications), strongly trip-oriented (less useful for "places I've been over my whole life"), the social feed is opt-in but visibly present.

Visited: the country/city checklist

Visited is the lightest of the three to start with. The mental model: a list of countries you've been to, expressed as percentages of the world map filled in. It also covers states, regions and cities, with 150+ lists to check off. It is free to download, and several features are paid in-app purchases: cities, regions, itineraries and ad removal are sold separately, and a Pro tier is listed at $59.99.

Strengths: low friction (mark a country as visited and you're done), good for travel braggability stats ("I've been to 47 countries").

Limitations: it is a checklist more than a record of individual places (a restaurant or a landmark), it has no flight log, and syncing across devices needs an account on its servers.

GlobePin: the personal travel record

GlobePin is what I'd call the "Letterboxd model" for travel: a private record of every individual place you've been (a city, a landmark, a specific restaurant), every flight you've taken, and every destination you're planning. The map is the primary view, with a 3D globe that shows all your pins. Stats cover countries, continents, nights abroad, furthest point and total miles, and the app also does trip planning, photo postcards and Travel Reels (a video of a trip or a year).

Strengths: works for a complete life travel record, not just one trip. iCloud sync without a separate account. No social feed, no algorithm. Visualization-first.

Limitations: it is the newest and least proven of the three (version 1.2, with two ratings on the App Store as of October 4, 2026). The free version shows ads, and a one-time $14.99 Pro purchase removes them. There is no automatic location tracking, so you add pins manually. (Deliberate: it's a record-keeping tool, not a tracking tool.)

Privacy posture

This is where the three apps diverge most sharply:

  • Polarsteps keeps your trips on its own servers, including your recorded route. Its App Store privacy label lists Contact Info as linked to you, and I could not confirm from its support pages whether an account is required.
  • Visited says it does not track your location, and that signing up saves your data on its servers so you can reach it from any device. Lower-stakes data than a GPS trail.
  • GlobePin uses Apple's CloudKit (iCloud) for sync. Your travel data lives in your own iCloud, not on Purplelink's servers. There is no Purplelink account. The free version does show ads served by Google AdMob, and the App Store privacy label lists identifiers and usage data under data used to track you, so read that label before you decide.

For most users this difference is invisible. For users in security-sensitive professions (journalists, lawyers, government, certain executives), where your travel pattern is itself sensitive, the architectural difference is meaningful.

How to choose

  • Pick Polarsteps if you want to journal individual trips and you're comfortable with background GPS and a Polarsteps account.
  • Pick Visited if you mostly want a country/city checklist and quick percentage stats.
  • Pick GlobePin if you want a complete lifetime travel record with both places and flights, you prefer no separate account, and the 3D globe view appeals.

Status of each

All three are live on the App Store. Polarsteps and Visited have years of releases behind them; GlobePin 1.0 shipped on 3 September 2026, so it is the new one here and has none of their track record. It is free to download, with ads, which makes it cheap to try alongside whichever you already use.
