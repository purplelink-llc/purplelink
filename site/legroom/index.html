What it does

It shows free space where you can see it

The menu bar shows what is free on your startup disk. The window under it adds the space available now, the space macOS says it can clear, and your other volumes. You set a line, and Legroom warns you when free space drops below it.

It cleans what you tell it to

You make a rule for a folder or a cache. The rule shows a preview of exactly what it would remove, and you approve it. A rule can then run when you ask, on a schedule, or when free space drops below your line. Each rule either moves items to the Trash or deletes them, and says which.

It shows what grew

Once a day Legroom measures where your space goes. What Grew compares those measurements over 1, 7 or 30 days, so you can see which folder filled the disk and make a rule for it.

What grew since last week

Each folder that grew is listed with its size and its growth, and a button for the next step: clean it now, add a rule, preview a rule or see a command. Items that come from macOS, such as swap and local Time Machine snapshots, are listed separately with what Legroom can and cannot tell you about them.

Suggested rules, weekly or monthly

Legroom looks at what grew, at your rules and at its list of known caches, and suggests rules. A suggestion never creates, approves or runs a rule. You see the rule editor and the preview first.

Space Map: where the space went

A chart of a folder or a whole disk, as rings: the centre is the folder you are in, and each ring outward is the folders inside it. Click a section to select it and double-click to look inside. A selected folder can be revealed in Finder, moved to the Trash or turned into a rule. Folders Legroom could not read are drawn hatched, so a gap is never passed off as empty space.

Find Files: large, old and duplicate files

Search your home folder, or folders you choose, for large files, files you have not opened in a long time, and duplicates. For duplicates you choose which copy stays, and at least one copy of every group always stays. Find Files only moves files to the Trash, and only the ones you select.

What it does not do

SpeedIt does not make your Mac faster. It frees disk space and does nothing else to performance.
System filesIt does not clean inside system folders and the folders that hold your documents and settings.
CommandsIt does not run commands. Where a fix needs Terminal, such as removing local Time Machine snapshots, it shows the command as text and you decide whether to run it.
Hidden spaceIt does not scan as an administrator, so some space it cannot measure, such as local Time Machine snapshots. It lists those as size not reported rather than guessing.
Unseen deletionsIt does not remove anything you have not previewed and approved, and it does not remove duplicates without leaving one copy.
The cloudThere is no cloud, no account and no sync. Your measurements, rules and log stay on your Mac.

Privacy

Legroom has no account and no analytics. Your daily measurements, your rules, the log of what was cleaned and the trial clock are stored on your Mac and are not sent anywhere. Apart from two things, Legroom does not use the network. The Buy button opens this page in your browser. And every copy, trial or licensed, asks this site once a day for its update feed: that request carries the app's name and version and an update token that is the same in every copy, plus what any web request carries, such as your IP address. It does not carry your files, your measurements or a profile of your Mac. Legroom asks for Full Disk Access only if you choose to give it, so it can read folders such as Desktop, Documents and Downloads. Details are on the privacy page.

Frequently asked questions

What does it need to run?
macOS 14 (Sonoma) or later, on Apple silicon or Intel.
Does it delete anything on its own?
Only through a rule you made, after you approved a preview of what the rule would remove. Each rule is set to move items to the Trash or to delete them permanently, and the preview says which. Space Map and Find Files only move items to the Trash, and only the ones you select.
Does it make my Mac faster?
No. Legroom frees disk space. It does not tune or speed up anything else.
Does it need Full Disk Access?
No. It asks only if you choose to give it. Without it, Legroom skips the folders macOS protects, such as Desktop, Documents and Downloads, and says which ones it could not read.
Does it send my files or measurements anywhere?
No. There is no account and no analytics. Apart from the download itself, the daily update check that every copy makes and the Buy link, which opens this page in your browser, Legroom does not use the network.
What happens when the trial ends?
The trial runs for seven days from the first time you open it, with no account. After that the menu-bar readout and the low-space alerts keep working. Cleaning rules, What Grew, Suggestions, Space Map and Find Files stop until you enter a license key. The key arrives in your receipt email and is shown on the success page, and the Find a purchase page sends it again. Choose Enter license key… from the menu-bar dropdown, or Settings, General, and paste it. It unlocks Legroom for good, offline, with no account and no second download.
I already bought Legroom. Do I need a key?
No. If you install this version over a copy you already paid for, it recognizes that copy and never asks for a key.
Are updates included?
Updates to the version you buy are included. Every copy, trial or licensed, checks for a new version once a day and asks before installing it, or you can check by hand from its menu. I have not decided whether a later major version would be a free update.
Can I get a refund?
Email ben@purplelink.llc within 14 days of purchase. See the terms.
Can I see free space without an app, or with a free one?
Yes. Finder's status bar, System Settings and Disk Utility show it on request, and the free app Stats can put it in the menu bar. The guide Show free disk space in the Mac menu bar walks through each and compares the menu-bar apps, including this one.
How do I get help?
