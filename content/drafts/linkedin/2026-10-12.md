## Option 1

This weekend I added a pricing option to the Mac Suite that could have cost me money if I got the math wrong.

The Suite is six apps, ModernTex, Outbound Veil, Legroom, Keyfeel, Tapefolio and a lifetime Vitae Plus key, for $54.99. Some people only want two or three of them. Now you can pick any two parts for $34.99, or any three for $44.99, the same price no matter which ones you choose.

The real work was making sure that pricing never beats the Suite. If four parts cost less than six, nobody would buy the full Suite again, and the bundle would eat its own value. So the per-part pricing only goes up to three parts. At four or more, the page points you to the Suite instead, because by then it is the better deal, and I would rather tell you that than let a loophole I built myself cost you money.

The server computes the price from the part count alone, so nothing sent from a browser can change what gets charged. I wrote 25 tests just to hold that one guarantee.

It is live at https://purplelink.llc/suite/.

I would like to hear about a pricing page you have found that let you pay more than you needed to.

## Option 2

ModernTex has been a one-key, one-person product since launch. This week I added checkout for labs and departments buying several seats at once.

The price steps down as the count goes up: 5 seats at $13.99 each, 10 at $12.49, 15 at $11.49, 20 at $10.49, and anything from 25 to 500 seats at $9.99 each. Every seat gets its own license key. A department handing one shared key to twenty people is the kind of arrangement that works fine until someone leaves or a Mac gets wiped, and then nobody can tell whose key is whose.

The harder part was making the numbers agree with each other. The price a buyer sees, the amount Stripe actually charges, and the keys that get issued all have to come from the same seat count read off the session itself, never from a number typed into a form that someone could alter before submitting. I wrote a small module that is the only place seat pricing lives, and eight tests that check the math holds at every tier.

Most researchers using ModernTex will never need more than one key. But a few departments asked, and building the real thing once seemed better than telling them to buy five separate licenses by hand.

It is at https://purplelink.llc/moderntex/#departments.

I would like to hear whether your department has ever bought software license by license, and what that process looked like.

## Option 3

Purplelink's traffic dashboard had been quietly wrong for a while, in Purplelink's favor.

I launched RubbleFront, a tactics game on a separate codebase, and pointed its page analytics at the same collector Purplelink's own site uses, to avoid running two systems. That worked, but it meant every RubbleFront page view, click and download landed in the same bucket as everyone who visits a purplelink.llc page. My monthly totals, top pages and referrer list were all a little inflated by traffic for a game that has not even launched yet.

The fix was having the collector check the host on every record. RubbleFront traffic is excluded from Purplelink's default numbers now, and the dashboard reports how many records got filtered out, so the exclusion is visible instead of silent. A separate query pulls RubbleFront's own numbers on their own, including counts like Steam clicks and downloads, so that data still exists, just kept where it belongs.

Nobody but me would have noticed the inflated numbers. I noticed anyway, because the only reason to look at a traffic dashboard is to make a real decision from it, and a decision made from numbers that are quietly wrong is worse than making no decision at all.

I would like to hear about a metric that turned out to be lying to you after you had already trusted it for a while.

## Selected
Option 2 -- it is the most concrete and specific to Purplelink's actual audience: a real pricing and engineering decision (seat-based licensing for academic labs and departments) that shows both craftsmanship (the single source of truth for pricing, tested at every tier) and honesty (one key per seat instead of a shared key that quietly breaks), told in plain terms a department buyer or researcher would recognize.
