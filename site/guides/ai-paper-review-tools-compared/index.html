AI paper review tools compared: what each one actually checks

By Benjamin Ampel · September 26, 2026

"AI paper review" covers three different jobs: language editing (Writefull), journal-format compliance (Paperpal Preflight), and methods or argument critique (SciScore, Purplelink Paper Review). Most manuscripts need more than one.

Disclosure up front: Purplelink makes Paper Review, one of the tools compared here. I have tried to write the other entries the way I would want to read them if I were choosing between them, using each vendor's own pricing and product pages. Prices and plans change; check the vendor's site before you pay.

Three different jobs, one search term

People search for "AI paper review tool" wanting very different things, and the tools that come up answer to different parts of that phrase.

  • Language editing. Grammar, word choice, sentence structure, whether the prose reads as native academic English. This is what a tool like Writefull does. It has no opinion on whether your methods are sound.
  • Journal-format compliance. Word and abstract limits, required sections, reference style, whether the manuscript matches the target journal's own submission checklist. This is Paperpal Preflight's job, and it is also language-aware.
  • Methods, statistics and argument critique. Whether the study design supports the claims, whether the statistics were reported the way the field expects, whether references actually say what you cited them for. SciScore does a narrow, deep version of this for biomedical methods sections. Paper Review does a broader version across fields, plus citation verification and figure checks.

None of the tools below does all three well. Below is what each one actually checks, what it costs, and what it misses.

Purplelink Paper Review

Paper Review reads an uploaded PDF with four AI personas in parallel: Methodology Critic, Statistical Skeptic, Data Integrity Officer and Editor-in-Chief. Every reference is checked against CrossRef, and for the most load-bearing claims the cited source's own abstract is fetched and compared against what you wrote, with a verdict of supported, partially supported, not supported by the abstract, contradicted, or source unavailable. A vision pass looks at every page for figure problems: broken axes, missing error bars, color-only encoding. Every tier includes an anonymity scan for double-blind submission. The output is a Markdown report with an A/B/C rectification checklist and an annotated PDF with comments on the pages they refer to. Choose it if you want one systematic pass across methods, statistics, citations and figures, in any field, and you are close to submitting.

Paperpal Preflight

Preflight is built for the last mile: does this manuscript match what the target journal actually asks for. It checks word and abstract limits, required sections, figure and table formatting, and reference style against the journal you pick, and Paperpal's broader language tools run alongside it. It ships as part of Paperpal Prime, a subscription that also includes general AI academic writing and editing features. Some journals have licensed Preflight directly and put a free or discounted scan in front of authors during submission, which is worth checking before you pay for Prime yourself. Choose it if your biggest risk is a desk rejection over formatting, not the substance of the argument.

SciScore

SciScore reads only the methods section, against reporting frameworks built for life-science research: NIH rigor criteria, the MDAR framework, and ARRIVE for animal studies. It flags missing detail on blinding, randomization, sample-size justification and sex as a biological variable, and checks that resources such as antibodies, cell lines, plasmids and organisms carry a resolvable identifier (an RRID) rather than a vague name. Several journals, including the FASEB family, run it automatically during submission and offer it to authors at no cost; individual researchers can also get 10 free reports a year with an ORCID account. Outside biomedicine, there is little for it to check. Choose it if you run wet-lab or animal research and want to catch a rigor-reporting gap before a reviewer does.

Writefull

Writefull is a language tool, not a review tool, and it does not claim otherwise. It works inside Word and inside Overleaf, understands LaTeX syntax well enough to leave commands alone, and suggests grammar, word choice and style edits trained on published journal articles, plus utilities like an abstract generator and a title generator. It has no opinion on your methods, your statistics or whether a citation supports the sentence it is attached to. Choose it if your manuscript's substance is solid and what is left is making the English read like a native academic wrote it, especially if you already work in Overleaf.

A general AI chatbot

Pasting a manuscript into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini and asking for a critique is free, fast and flexible: you can ask it to focus on anything. What you lose is structure. There is no fixed report format, no guarantee every reference gets checked (most assistants will not look one up unless you ask, and then only if they have a working search tool), and a long paper can be read unevenly if it is pasted in one piece. It can also state something wrong with complete confidence. Choose it if you want a fast first read on clarity and argument, and you plan to verify anything it tells you against the actual data and literature yourself.

The one you can't buy: StatReviewer

StatReviewer deserves a mention because it turns up in searches for this topic, but it belongs to a different category from everything above. It runs automated statistical-reporting checks (against frameworks such as CONSORT) inside a journal's own editorial system, typically Editorial Manager, after you submit, not before. A handful of publishers, including some BMC journals, use it alongside human peer review. There is no self-serve way for an individual author to buy a StatReviewer report ahead of time; if your target journal uses it, you will meet it as part of the review process rather than as a tool you choose.

Which to choose

  • Formatting risk for a specific journal? Paperpal Preflight, or check whether your target journal already licenses it for free.
  • Wet-lab or animal-study methods section? SciScore, especially if it is free through your target journal.
  • Prose needs a native-English pass? Writefull, in whichever of Word or Overleaf you already write in.
  • Want a fast, free, unstructured first look? A general AI chatbot, then verify anything it flags.
  • Want one systematic pass on methods, statistics, citations and figures, in any field? Paper Review, from $9.

Most manuscripts benefit from more than one of these, and none of them replaces a colleague or advisor who knows your subfield. For how to sequence AI tools with human readers, preprints and editing services, see how to get feedback on a paper before submitting.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best AI tool for reviewing a paper before submission?
It depends on what you need checked. Paperpal Preflight is built for formatting and journal-specific submission requirements plus language. SciScore is built for methods-section rigor in biomedical and life-science papers. Purplelink Paper Review covers methods and statistics red flags, citation support, figure integrity and an anonymity scan in one report, across fields. None of them replaces a human reader who knows your subfield.
Is Paperpal Preflight free?
Not on its own. Preflight is bundled into a paid Paperpal Prime subscription. Some journals license Preflight directly and offer authors a free or discounted scan through their own submission system, so check your target journal's author guidelines before paying.
Does SciScore work for papers outside biomedicine?
Not really. SciScore checks a methods section against reporting standards built for life-science research (NIH rigor criteria, MDAR, ARRIVE for animal studies) and looks for identifiers on resources such as antibodies, cell lines and organisms. A computer science, engineering or social science paper has none of that, so SciScore has little to check.
Can I just paste my paper into ChatGPT or Claude for a review?
You can, and it often produces useful comments on clarity and argument in minutes. It is weaker for systematic work: a long manuscript can be read unevenly, references are not checked against a bibliographic database unless the assistant has a lookup tool and uses it for every entry, and it can state an error confidently. Whether your text may be used for training depends on the product, the plan and your settings, so check those before pasting unpublished work.
What happens to my manuscript if I use Purplelink Paper Review?
It is sent to Anthropic's Claude API for analysis. Anthropic retains inputs for up to 30 days for abuse monitoring and does not train on them. Purplelink deletes the manuscript and the review from its own servers 30 minutes after you first open the result, or after 24 hours if you never do, so save the report before closing the tab.
Is an AI paper review a substitute for peer review?
No. These tools check the parts of a manuscript that are systematic and easy to skip under deadline: formatting, references, reporting completeness, statistics red flags. None of them can tell you whether your contribution matters to the field the way an expert reviewer can, and all of them can be wrong. Use one alongside at least one human reader who knows your area.
