Why is System Data so big on my Mac? What fills it and what is safe to remove

By Benjamin Ampel · October 12, 2026, updated October 19, 2026

System Data is a leftover category, not a folder, so there is nothing to delete as a whole. Most of the space comes from a short list of causes. Some are safe to clear, and others belong to macOS. This guide shows how to see which one is yours and what to do about it.

Disclosure up front: Purplelink, which publishes this site, makes one app mentioned here, Legroom, a menu-bar app sold as a 7-day trial and a $9.99 one-time purchase. It is young and does not clear the biggest suspects below, and I say where other tools do better. I ran the commands and looked at the folders named here on my own Mac on October 12, 2026 (macOS 27.2). I read everything else from Apple's pages and the vendors' own pages on the same day. Where a page did not say something, I write "not stated" and do not guess.

The short answer

Apple describes System Data as a general category. The category measures the storage space used by all Apple and third-party files that do not belong to a more specific category (Apple's page on freeing up storage, published July 6, 2026). It is whatever macOS does not file under Applications, Documents, Photos and the other labelled bars. It can hold app data, caches, Time Machine local snapshots, device backups and system files at once.

To find your cause, open System Settings, General, Storage. Then look inside your Library folder for the biggest folders, as the next section shows. The usual suspects are app data and caches, Time Machine local snapshots, old iPhone or iPad backups, and the models behind Apple Intelligence. A media analysis cache that some people find at 100 GB or more is another. None of the tools in this guide shows System Data as one tidy item either, so expect to work through folders.

Find where it went

  1. Look at Apple's bar. In macOS 13 or later, choose Apple menu, System Settings, General, Storage (Apple's Optimize Storage page). The System Data number there is a computed leftover. It can differ from what you add up folder by folder in Finder.
  2. Open your Library folder. In Finder, hold the Option key and choose Library from the Go menu (Apple's page on going to a folder). Most of the user-level space is under it.
  3. Make Finder show folder sizes. In a window set to List view, choose View, Show View Options (Command-J) and tick Calculate All Sizes (MacRumors describes the steps). Finder remembers the setting per folder. Calculating every size can be slow.
  4. Or ask Terminal. This lists the ten biggest folders directly under your Library. It took about 40 seconds on my Mac: du -sh ~/Library/* 2>/dev/null | sort -h | tail -10. It only reads and deletes nothing.

Not everything is under your own Library. Time Machine snapshots and the sleep image live elsewhere, as the next section explains.

Time Machine local snapshots

This is the usual reason deleting files does not free space. Apple says Time Machine also stores some of its backups on your Mac, and that your Mac counts the space used by snapshots as available storage. Time Machine deletes them as they age or as space is needed. To see them, run tmutil listlocalsnapshots / in Terminal. On my Mac it listed none. Apple's documented way to remove them by hand is to turn off automatic backups in Time Machine settings and wait a few minutes. The tmutil man page also lists deletelocalsnapshots and thinlocalsnapshots. I did not run either.

iPhone and iPad backups

Finder stores device backups at ~/Library/Application Support/MobileSync/Backup. Apple's page on locating and managing backups says to manage them from the device in Finder. Choose General, then Manage Backups, to delete, archive or reveal a backup. That folder was empty on my Mac because I do not back up devices to it. Do not delete the folder's contents by hand unless you are sure you do not need the backup, because a deleted backup cannot be restored.

Apple Intelligence models

Apple's page on Apple Intelligence requirements, published September 14, 2026, lists up to 14 GB of storage for Apple Intelligence on a Mac with an M3 or later. That Mac also needs at least 12 GB of unified memory (the memory the CPU and GPU share). Other supported devices need up to 8 GB. 9to5Mac reported on October 5, 2026 that Apple Intelligence needed up to 7 GB on macOS 26 and that Apple says up to 14 GB on macOS 27. It also reported that an open-source tool called RemoveMacAI claims to reclaim 12 GB or more. I have not run that tool. The Apple page I read does not describe a way to remove the models while keeping the feature, so I do not recommend removing them by hand.

The media analysis cache

Photos and related features use a background process called mediaanalysisd, which keeps a cache in a container folder. Michael Tsai's blog collects reports of it at 15 GB or more, with some people reporting 50, 80 or 140 GB, and says the cause is unknown. In an Apple Community thread from May 2025, one person deleted a 21 GB subfolder and reported freeing about 30 GB. Other replies advised deleting it, and nobody there reported a problem. A popular r/MacOS thread this week reported 137 GB in the same place (thread, read October 12, 2026).

Apple has published nothing about this folder, and I could not confirm from those pages whether macOS rebuilds what you delete. So this is a report, not advice. If you try it, quit the apps that use Photos, remove only what is inside the cache folder, and leave the container folder in place. On my Mac the folder is 491 MB and holds different files from the ones those reports describe. A big number is not universal.

Photos sent to you in Messages

Messages keeps the photos and videos people send you in a separate, hidden Photos library, Syndication.photoslibrary, which feeds Shared with You. Howard Oakley at Eclectic Light (December 12, 2025) found it can keep items Messages no longer shows, and advises against deleting it. An r/MacOS post in October 2026 reported 8 GB, and turning off Shared with You did not shrink it, because that only stops new items. On my Mac it is 3.2 GB.

To shrink it, remove the attachments in Messages itself. Apple's page on deleting messages on Mac describes both ways: delete items from a conversation's Photos list, or choose Messages, Settings, General and set Keep messages to something other than Forever, which removes older conversations and their attachments. If Messages in iCloud is on, deletions reach your other devices too, so save anything you want into Photos first.

What to leave alone

Do not touch /System, and do not delete swap or the sleep image in /private/var/vm. The sleep image on my Mac is 2 GB. macOS manages it. If you cannot name what a folder is, find out before you remove it.

What I measured on my own Mac

For scale, this is a Mac running macOS 27.2 with a 228 GB startup disk and 52 GB free. Under my Library: Application Support 11 GB, Group Containers 7.4 GB, Containers 4.8 GB, Caches 3.5 GB, the media analysis cache 491 MB, the Messages photo library 3.2 GB (measured October 19, 2026), and device backups none. It is an ordinary working Mac, and none of its folders is anywhere near the 100 GB reports. System Data is large on some Macs because of one specific thing. The folders above are how you find out which.

Where Legroom fits, and where it does not

Legroom lives in the menu bar and shows free space on the startup disk. Its daily "what grew" comparison over 1, 7 or 30 days names the folders that filled the disk, which helps when something quietly grew over a week. Cleaning is by rules you make, and each rule previews what it would remove before you approve it. Its Space Map draws folders as rings.

The limits matter for this problem. Legroom does not offer Apple's media analysis cache for cleaning, and it does not remove Apple Intelligence models. It does not scan as an administrator, so it reports space such as local Time Machine snapshots as not measured. It shows the Terminal command as text and does not run it. Legroom will therefore not find or clear the 100 GB folder from the reports above. A folder map such as DaisyDisk will find it, and you then decide what to do. Pick Legroom if you want a free-space readout, a view of what grew, and cleaning rules you approve first, and you accept a young product from a one-person studio.

Frequently asked

What is System Data on a Mac?

Apple describes System Data as a general category. The category measures the storage space used by all Apple and third-party files that do not belong to a more specific category (Apple's page on freeing up storage, published July 6, 2026). It is not one folder you can delete. It is whatever macOS does not file under Applications, Documents, Photos or the other labelled categories.

Can I delete System Data?

Not as a whole, because it is not a single item. Some things that land in it are safe to remove, such as old device backups and the app data of apps you have already uninstalled. Others, such as swap and the sleep image, should be left to macOS. This guide lists each common cause and whether it is safe to remove.

Why did System Data grow to more than 100 GB?

People report five causes most often: a large media analysis cache, many Time Machine local snapshots, old iPhone or iPad backups, orphaned app data and, on newer Macs, the models Apple Intelligence needs. Apple lists up to 14 GB for Apple Intelligence on Macs with an M3 or later. The media analysis cache has been reported at 15 GB to 140 GB, but Apple has published nothing on it and the cause is unknown.

Is it safe to delete the mediaanalysisd cache?

Apple does not document it, so I cannot say it is safe. People in an Apple Community thread report deleting it and freeing tens of gigabytes, and nobody there reported a problem. However, that is not Apple's guidance. If you try it, quit the apps that use Photos first and remove only the contents of the cache folder, not the container itself.

Does Legroom clear System Data?

Partly, and not the biggest suspects. Legroom shows which folders grew over the last 1, 7 or 30 days and previews each cleaning rule before anything is removed. It does not offer Apple's media analysis cache for cleaning, it does not remove Apple Intelligence models, and it reports Time Machine snapshot space as not measured.

What is Syndication.photoslibrary, and can I delete it?

It is a hidden Photos library in your Library folder that holds photos and videos people sent you in Messages, for Shared with You. Do not delete it by hand: macOS rebuilds it, and Messages media can go with it. Shrink it by deleting large attachments in Messages, or by setting Keep messages to 1 year. Turning off Shared with You only stops new items being added.
