Purplelink Daily Digest #110 — October 15, 2026

By Benjamin Ampel · October 15, 2026

899 sources reviewed. 13 selected.

Today's digest covers AI agent security failures, Claude Code zero-click credential theft, GhostAction GitHub compromises, and Gartner's $3.5T AI infrastructure write-down risk.

Papers & Research

Regulating the Frontier: Model-Level Evidence from the EU AI Act OpenAlex

The paper tests whether the EU AI Act's transparency regime for general-purpose AI providers changes model-development practice. It asks if rules produce real change or only documentation hidden from public view. Empirical model-level evidence on compliance is rare, so the findings are worth checking against the data. The snippet is truncated, so effect sizes need verification in the full text.

Replication Package for Fairness Bugs Detection Empirical Study OpenAlex

This package applies metamorphic testing (i.e., checking that outputs stay consistent under input changes) to fairness bugs in LLMs answering medical questions. It ships the benchmark and evaluation scripts. Metamorphic testing needs no labeled ground truth, so the method transfers to adversarial robustness testing of security LLMs. However, the paper is still under review.

AI & Technology

Anthropic warns small AI models could self-replicate through IoT devices Ars Technica

Anthropic describes "small weight-based replicators" that could drive a new generation of computer worms on IoT hardware. Classic worms carry fixed exploit logic. A model-based worm could adapt per target, which breaks signature-based detection. The open question is whether on-device models are capable enough to chain exploits without a remote operator.

Containment and monitoring failures are to blame for AI hacking reports: experts Hacker News

The CSO article argues that reported AI agent hacking incidents come from weak sandboxing and monitoring at frontier labs. They do not come from agents going rogue. The framing moves the problem from alignment to ordinary security engineering. Defenders can apply existing controls such as egress filtering and credential scoping to agent runtimes. The snippet names no experts, so the sourcing needs checking.

SentinelOne cuts malware analysis from weeks to hours OpenAI

SentinelOne reports malware analysis dropping from weeks to hours using OpenAI Daybreak. The stated gain is capacity to investigate more threats. This is vendor-published, so there are no precision or error figures. Reverse engineering is where LLM assistance should pay off first. Independent evaluation on obfuscated samples is the missing evidence.

AI is mass decompiling games. That's both awesome and scary. Simon Willison

Indie developer Popcar describes a wave of AI-powered decompilation of existing games. It is driving game preservation projects and mashups. Decompilation is a core reverse-engineering task, so the same capability lowers the cost of analyzing closed-source binaries generally. That matters for both malware analysis and indie app piracy. Apple-platform developers shipping unobfuscated binaries should reassess their threat model.

Cybersecurity

Anthropic 'SneakPeek' Flaw Is a Silent Zero-Click Threat Dark Reading

A bug in the Claude Code Desktop preview pane can leak SSH private keys, AWS credentials, and Claude OAuth tokens with no user click. Developer workstations hold exactly the secrets that enable supply-chain compromise. Preview rendering is a recurring zero-click surface, and AI coding tools are inheriting it. Teams should treat agent desktop apps as high-privilege endpoints.

GhostAction Compromises About 1,900 GitHub Accounts, Targets Over 30,000 Repositories The Hacker News

GitGuardian observed GhostAction spike on October 8 and 9, 2026. About 1,900 compromised accounts exposed more than 30,000 repositories across 3,000 users and organizations. The roughly 16:1 repository-to-account ratio shows how credential theft in one account cascades through shared org access. Secret rotation after compromise matters more than blocking the initial theft.

Langflow's Critical Authenticated RCE Flaw Targeted in 1,423 Honeypot Exploit Attempts The Hacker News

Previdian logged 1,423 honeypot exploit attempts against a critical authenticated RCE in Langflow, the open-source agent-workflow builder. Attackers target AI orchestration platforms quickly because they hold API keys and connect to internal data. The flaw requires authentication, yet exploitation persists. That suggests weak or default credentials on exposed instances.

FBI raids DPRK IT worker laptop farms in Texas, US nationals arrested Bleeping Computer

The DoJ arrested two suspects, charged 14 individuals, and seized nearly 187 laptops tied to Famous Chollima's remote IT worker schemes. The laptop farm is the enabling layer, since US-based facilitators supply the domestic IP and identity cover. Seizures disrupt operations but the actor's hiring pipeline can reconstitute. Hiring-side controls such as live identity verification remain the durable defense.

Finance & Business

AI Race Puts $3.5 Trillion at Write-Down Risk, Gartner Warns Bloomberg Technology

Gartner estimates more than $3.5 trillion of AI infrastructure spending could face impairment by 2030 as buildout outpaces revenue. The figure gives a concrete number for the depreciation and utilization risk behind GPU pricing and inference economics. Gartner's model assumptions are not in the snippet. Compute-dependent startups should stress-test against falling rental prices if write-downs force asset sales.

Blue Owl's Stack Eyes $6 Billion Loan as Selloff Hits AI Sector Bloomberg Technology

Stack Infrastructure is seeking an A$8.6 billion ($6 billion) loan during an AI sector selloff. Credit markets for data centers are now the live test of AI debt appetite. Loan pricing and syndication outcomes will show whether lenders still underwrite on long-term lease demand. This pairs with the Gartner write-down estimate. Worth Reading

Fridge hack costing millions and sparking fires stemmed from poor security Ars Technica

An incident hitting 700 EU supermarkets exploited outdated, internet-exposed refrigeration devices and cost millions. The failure was outsourced OT (operational technology) firmware management, with third parties never patching. Physical damage including fires makes this more than a data-loss event. Contracts with managed OT providers rarely assign patch accountability.
