The biomedical profile

What the reviewers look for

These are the instructions the profile adds to the review, in plain words. Each finding in the report quotes the passage it is about and says what would fix it.

  • Sample size. Whether a power calculation was actually done, or the sample size came from convention.
  • Multiple comparisons. Corrections such as Bonferroni or FDR missing where many tests were run, and p-values clustered just under 0.05.
  • Image integrity. Western blot and gel bands that look spliced or repeated, or that do not match the loading control; "representative" images with no quantification.
  • Tests and survival curves. Parametric tests on small or non-normal samples, and survival curves without a log-rank test or censoring disclosure.
  • Blinding and randomization. Whether the methods show them, or only assert them.
  • Registration. For trials, whether the trial is registered and the reported primary endpoint matches the registered one.
  • Ethics and conflicts. IRB or IACUC statements that are specific, and conflicts of interest behind industry-funded efficacy claims.
  • Clinical relevance. Statistically significant effects that are too small to matter clinically, common in large-N studies.

What a finding looks like

An example from the report

Illustrative, not from a real manuscript: this is the shape each finding takes. The full sample report shows every section.

No power calculation for the animal cohorts.
Quoted: "n = 8 mice per group, consistent with previous studies."
Surfaced by: Statistical Skeptic.
Fix: Report the power calculation that set n, or the smallest effect 8 per group can detect at 80% power, and give confidence intervals alongside the p-values.

In every review

The rest of the report

  • Four reviewers: a Methodology Critic, a Statistical Skeptic, a Data Integrity Officer and an Editor-in-Chief, with findings more than one of them raise listed first.
  • Every reference checked against CrossRef, and for the most important claims, the cited paper's abstract read to see whether it supports the sentence citing it.
  • A figure and table scan for integrity and presentation problems.
  • An anonymity scan for double-blind submission.
  • A prioritized checklist, as a Markdown report and an annotated PDF with comments on the pages they refer to.
  • The Journal Pack ($11) adds a check against one venue's submission rules. In biomedicine it covers Nature Medicine, JAMA, NEJM, The Lancet and The BMJ, plus Nature, Science, Cell and PLOS ONE. All 25 venues.

Questions

Can it read my figures?
Yes. Each review includes a scan of the figures and tables for integrity and presentation problems, alongside the text review.
How do I choose this profile?
Pay on the Paper Review page, then pick the domain on the upload page. Starting from this page selects it for you. There are five: Machine Learning, Biomedicine, Psychology / Social Science, Chemistry / Materials, and General.
What happens to the manuscript?
It is sent to Anthropic's Claude API for the review and deleted from Purplelink's storage 30 minutes after you first open the result, or after 24 hours if you never do. Anthropic keeps API inputs for up to 30 days for abuse monitoring and does not train on them.
How long does it take and what does it cost?
Standard is $9 and the Journal Pack $11, usually back in 4 to 8 minutes. Deep Review is $15 and takes 10 to 15. There is no subscription or account.
