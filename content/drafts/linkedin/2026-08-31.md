## Option 1

The most costly literature gap is not a paper you forgot to check. It is one you could not have found.

A machine learning researcher searching for "distributional shift" will not surface a psychometrics paper on "test-retest reliability across populations," even if both papers address the same structural problem. The vocabularies do not overlap. Neither author is searching the other's terminology. The gap is invisible until a reviewer cites the paper you missed.

This failure mode is distinct from carelessness. You ran a thorough search. You followed citation trails. You asked colleagues. The adjacent-field paper still did not appear, because you had no way to know which adjacent field to check.

There is also a timing problem. A paper published while your manuscript was under revision is recent enough to be known to reviewers but too recent for a literature search you did six months before submission.

I published a blog post this week on both problems and how Citation Gap addresses them. The tool reads a manuscript, identifies what the paper is actually claiming to contribute, and searches for prior work semantically -- not by matching your search terms. Adjacent-field papers using different vocabulary for the same concept surface before a reviewer finds them for you.

The post is at purplelink.llc/blog/what-your-literature-search-misses/

If you have received a rejection that cited a paper you had never seen, I would be curious what field it came from.

## Option 2

I published a guide this week on formatting citations from DOIs and arXiv IDs using the Citation Generator at purplelink.llc. I want to explain one architectural decision behind the tool.

The generator runs entirely in your browser. When you paste a DOI, the page calls Crossref directly. For an arXiv ID, it calls the arXiv API. For an ISBN, Open Library. The metadata comes back, gets formatted into APA, MLA, Chicago, or IEEE, and displays in the page. Nothing is routed through Purplelink's servers.

This was a deliberate choice. A tool that handles DOIs through an intermediary server is collecting information about what papers you are citing, which implies something about what you are working on. That felt like unnecessary data collection for a problem that does not require it. The public APIs are available directly from a browser. There was no technical reason to add a server round-trip.

The practical result: paste a DOI, an arXiv ID, or a full doi.org or arxiv.org URL, pick a citation style, copy the output. If the identifier does not resolve, a manual entry form produces the same formatted output.

One thing to check after generating: author names with diacritics occasionally encode incorrectly in source database records. Worth verifying those by hand before submitting.

Guide at purplelink.llc/guides/format-citation-from-doi-arxiv-id/

## Option 3

Today was Daily Digest issue 67.

The digest is a daily summary of research papers and technical content across five areas: academic papers, AI and machine learning, cybersecurity, finance, and entrepreneurship. Each issue is a static page with an RSS feed. No email subscription, no account required.

I started it as an internal pipeline to stay current across the fields that intersect with Purplelink's products and audience. Publishing it daily means making the same classification decisions consistently: what qualifies as notable versus routine, how to summarize a technical result without flattening it, which items belong in which topic area when a paper crosses categories. The discipline is mostly showing up and making those calls each day.

At 67 issues, I have a clearer sense of what draws readers. Research papers with a specific methodological contribution get more engagement than general tech news. Items that connect a finding to practice -- what does this change about how you work -- perform better than items that stop at describing a result.

Shipping a digest every day is a small, concrete commitment. It is also a forcing function for reading carefully: you cannot summarize something you only skimmed.

The current and archive issues are at purplelink.llc/digest/

If you follow any daily-digest-style publications in your field, I would be curious what keeps them worth reading over time.
