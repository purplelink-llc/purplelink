Dictation on a Mac that stays on the Mac

By Benjamin Ampel · October 23, 2026

Dictation apps differ less in what they type than in where your voice goes. Some turn speech into text on your Mac; others send the audio to a server. This guide says which is which, with each maker's own page as the source.

Disclosure up front: I make Tapefolio, which includes dictation, and it is in the table below. It is $29.99 once after a 7-day trial. I did not install or test the other apps. Their rows come from each maker's own pages, read on October 23, 2026. Prices change often in this category, so check before you buy. Where a page did not say something, the table says so instead of guessing.

The short answer

Start with Apple Dictation: it is free, it is already on your Mac, and Apple says requests can be processed on the device. If you want a custom word list, snippets or a different shortcut, VoiceInk is a one-time purchase that transcribes on the Mac and is open source. Wispr Flow is popular, but its own privacy page says transcription always happens in the cloud.

Match the app to the job

  • Free and good enough: Apple Dictation. Press the Dictation key or the shortcut you set in Keyboard settings. Apple says it has no time limit and stops after 30 seconds of silence.
  • One payment, open source, on the Mac: VoiceInk.
  • You also dictate on Windows or a phone: Superwhisper or Wispr Flow. With Wispr Flow, your voice goes to its servers.
  • You also transcribe interviews or calls: TranscribeX or Tapefolio, which do both in one app. Tapefolio adds speaker labels and replaces names in transcripts with codes; it is the newest and least proven app here.

What none of them fix

Every dictation app mishears names, places and technical words. A word list helps: VoiceInk has custom vocabulary, and Tapefolio has a dictionary. Read what was typed before you send it. An app that types for you also needs Accessibility permission in macOS; Tapefolio puts the text on the clipboard instead if you do not grant it.

Frequently asked

Is dictation on a Mac private?

Apple's Mac help says dictation requests can be processed on your device and not sent to its servers, and shows how to check for your language. Third-party apps differ: VoiceInk and Tapefolio transcribe on the Mac, and Wispr Flow says transcription always happens in the cloud.

Does Wispr Flow work offline?

Its privacy page says transcription always happens in the cloud, so plan on an internet connection. If you need dictation without one, Apple Dictation, VoiceInk, Superwhisper's offline models and Tapefolio work on the Mac.

Which Mac dictation apps are a one-time purchase?

VoiceInk ($25 for one Mac), TranscribeX Pro ($39) and Tapefolio ($29.99, which I make) are one-time purchases as of October 23, 2026. Apple Dictation is free. Wispr Flow is a subscription.
