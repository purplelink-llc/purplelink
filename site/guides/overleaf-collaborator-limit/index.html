Overleaf collaborator limit on the free plan: what you can still do

By Benjamin Ampel · November 27, 2026

Overleaf's free plan allows one collaborator per project. A collaborator is an Editor or a Reviewer you invite. Viewers do not count, and the free plan allows as many as you like. That leaves more room than the limit suggests, as long as one person does most of the typing.

Disclosure: I make ModernTex, a Mac LaTeX editor, and it comes up near the end. It does not solve this limit, and the section says why. The limits and roles below come from Overleaf's plans page, its premium features page, its guide to sharing a project and its page on reviewers, all read on November 27, 2026.

What counts toward the limit

The plans page lists "1 collaborator per project" for the free plan and defines a collaborator as "an Editor or Reviewer that you invite to work with you on a project." The premium features page puts both roles in one row and gives the free plan 1. Viewers get their own row, and the free plan's entry there is "unlimited."

Two details matter here. First, a Reviewer still uses the slot. The reviewers page says you can make your one invited collaborator a Reviewer on the free plan, "but they will only be able to comment." Second, link sharing is not a documented way around the limit. The sharing page says people who join through a link do not appear in the Manage access menu, and none of the pages I read say whether they count. Plan around named invitations.

Work within the limit, in this order

  1. Give the slot to the person who writes the most. Make them the Editor. Everyone else can be a Viewer, which costs nothing and lets them read the current draft and its PDF.
  2. Use a Reviewer only if comments are the whole job. A Reviewer comments but does not edit, and still takes the slot. For a supervisor who marks up drafts and never types, that is the right role. For a co-author, it is not.
  3. Collect comments from Viewers outside Overleaf. Viewers cannot comment in the project. Ask them to annotate the PDF or reply by email, and have the Editor make the changes.
  4. Move the slot between rounds. Overleaf's downgrade notes say you can assign edit access to one of your existing collaborators, which suggests the slot can be moved. I have not tested this. If it works for your project, give edit access to whichever co-author is drafting, and change it at each handover.
  5. Check your institution first. A site licence brings a paid plan at no cost to you, and with it a larger limit.

What a paid plan changes

The premium features page lists 10 collaborators on the Student and Standard plans and an unlimited number on Pro. Track changes is also a paid feature, which matters if your co-authors want to suggest edits rather than make them. Overleaf's plans page listed these prices in US dollars on November 27, 2026: Student $98 a year or $13 a month, Standard $199 a year or $25 a month, and Pro $399 a year or $45 a month, each with a 7-day trial. Some universities pay for Overleaf through a site licence, so ask your library before you pay.

If you drop back to the free plan

The premium features page says a downgraded account is "allowed only one collaborator on new and existing projects." It also says that if you downgrade first, "all collaborators will be moved to view-only permissions," after which you can give edit access back to one of them. Decide before you downgrade which co-author keeps edit access on each project.

Where a local editor fits

A local editor does not give you more Overleaf collaborators. It changes who needs one. If you write most of the paper, you can draft on your own machine and leave the Overleaf slot to the co-author who wants to type in the browser. The project is ordinary .tex, .bib and figure files, so moving changes in and out works on the free plan, by hand.

ModernTex is my option for that on a Mac. It is a single-user app, so two people cannot type in the same document at once, and it is not a substitute for Overleaf's live editing. For a co-author who already uses GitHub or GitLab, its Get Changes and Send Changes commands work with any plain Git repository, which has no Overleaf collaborator count at all. It costs $19.99 once after a free 7-day trial and needs macOS 14 or later. The free editors in the LaTeX editor for Mac comparison do the drafting part as well.

Frequently asked questions

How many collaborators does the Overleaf free plan allow?
One per project. Overleaf counts Editors and Reviewers you invite as collaborators. Viewers are unlimited on the free plan. This is from Overleaf's plans and premium features pages, read November 27, 2026.
Do Viewers count toward the Overleaf collaborator limit?
No. Overleaf's premium features page lists Viewers separately and gives the free plan an unlimited number. Viewers can read the project but cannot edit or comment.
Does a Reviewer count as a collaborator on Overleaf?
Yes. Editors and Reviewers share the one free slot. On the free plan a Reviewer can only comment, because track changes is a paid feature.
What happens to my collaborators if I downgrade from a paid Overleaf plan?
Overleaf's premium features page says you are allowed one collaborator on new and existing projects, and that downgrading first moves all collaborators to view-only. You can then give edit access back to one of them.
