Critical blind spots

  • Hyperparameter search asymmetry favors the proposed method.
    Quoted: "We tuned learning rate and batch size on the validation set; baselines used reported defaults."
    Surfaced by: Methodology Critic, Statistical Skeptic (consensus).
    Fix: Match the search budget for each baseline and report the protocol in the methods section.

Data-to-claim contradictions

  • Claim: "Our method beats all baselines by a wide margin."
    Evidence: Table 2 shows the proposed method tied with Baseline C within reported standard errors on 4 of 7 datasets.

Rectification checklist

  • A Add the hyperparameter search protocol to the methods (page 5, Section 3.2).
  • B Reference [17] only weakly matches its CrossRef entry; check the title and DOI.
  • C Figure 3's y-axis breaks 0-60 then jumps to 90; redraw on a continuous scale.

How the review works

  • MCMethodology CriticDesign, controls, confounds and what the method can and cannot show.
  • SSStatistical SkepticPower, tests, intervals and claims the numbers do not support.
  • DIData Integrity OfficerFigures, tables and whether the data shown matches the text.
  • ECEditor-in-ChiefNovelty, fit, structure and what a desk editor would send back.

Built by Benjamin Ampel, assistant professor of computer information systems at Georgia State University, with publications in MIS Quarterly and JMIS.

  1. Layer 1: figure-integrity scan. Every page is rendered and reviewed for digital manipulation concerns, figure-vs-text contradictions, and presentation issues (broken y-axes, missing error bars, color-only encoding). Hedged language; flags concerns to investigate, never accusations.
  2. Layer 2: citation support audit. Every reference is verified against CrossRef, then we go deeper: for the most load-bearing claims, we fetch the cited source's abstract and check whether it actually supports the claim you attached it to. Each finding shows your claim next to the source's own words, with a verdict: supported, partially supported, not supported by the abstract, contradicted, or source unavailable. Hedged language; verify each against the full text.
  3. Layer 3: a four-reviewer panel. Methodology Critic, Statistical Skeptic, Data Integrity Officer, and Editor-in-Chief each red-team the paper in parallel. Findings are merged with a cross-persona consensus filter so issues raised independently by multiple personas surface first.
  4. Layer 4: the report. A final pass produces the structured report you'll act on: critical blind spots, data-to-claim contradictions, an A/B/C rectification checklist, and an honest novelty estimate.

Frequently asked questions

What does the review include?
Four layers in sequence: a Claude Vision pass over your figures and tables, a live CrossRef cross-check of every reference, an adversarial four-persona reviewer debate (Methodology Critic, Statistical Skeptic, Data Integrity Officer, Editor-in-Chief) with consensus filtering, and a synthesis pass producing critical blind spots, data-to-claim contradictions, a prioritised rectification checklist, and a true-novelty estimate.
How long does it take?
Typically 4-8 minutes for a 20-page manuscript. You don't need to keep the tab in the foreground. The status page polls until the review is ready and renders it inline.
What about my privacy? Is the manuscript stored?
Your manuscript is sent to Anthropic's Claude API for analysis. Anthropic retains inputs for up to 30 days for abuse monitoring, and does not use them for model training. The report is emailed to you as a PDF when the review finishes. The manuscript and the online copy are deleted 30 minutes after you first open the result, or after 24 hours if you never open it. The email goes through Resend to the address on your Stripe receipt, or to the one you enter at upload. The manuscript is never attached.
Which journals does the Journal Pack check?

25 venues. The pack checks word and abstract limits, required sections and reference-style hints against the one you pick at upload.

  • Machine learning: NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, AAAI, TMLR, JMLR
  • General science: Nature, Science, Cell, PLOS ONE
  • Medicine: Nature Medicine, JAMA, NEJM, The Lancet, The BMJ
  • Psychology and management: Psychological Science, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, Cognition, Academy of Management Journal
  • Chemistry and materials: JACS, Angewandte Chemie, Nature Materials, Nature Chemistry, ACS Nano, Advanced Materials

Not listed? The Standard tier covers everything except this check. Email to ask for a venue.

Is there a referral credit?
Yes. Every report ends with a link carrying your own code. When a colleague opens it and buys a review with a .edu email address, you each receive a single-use $2 code by email. The link counts for 30 days in the browser that opened it.
What domains does it cover?
You pick one of five domain profiles at upload: Machine Learning, Biomedicine, Psychology and Social Science, Chemistry and Materials, or General. Each profile tells the reviewers which failure modes matter most in that field; each link lists them.
How deep is the citation check?
Two levels. First, every reference is checked for existence against CrossRef (dead DOIs, hallucinated or weakly-matched titles). Second, for the most load-bearing claims we fetch the cited source's abstract from OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, or CrossRef and assess whether it supports your claim. This is an abstract-level check: a claim supported only in the full text is marked "not supported by abstract," not wrong. Verify it against the source. When no abstract can be retrieved, the citation is marked "source unavailable" rather than guessed. On citation-heavy papers we audit the most important claims first and tell you how many were not audited.
Am I allowed to use an AI review on my own manuscript?
Yes, under the policies of the major publishers and venues we have checked. Elsevier, Springer Nature, Wiley, Taylor & Francis, IEEE, ACM, ICMJE and NeurIPS let authors use AI tools while preparing a manuscript; the rule against uploading manuscripts to AI is for reviewers, who hold someone else's work. Paper Review writes nothing into your paper, so most disclosure rules, which cover AI-generated text, do not obviously apply; if your venue's policy is strict, a one-line note is enough. The policy guide compares ten publishers and venues.
Is this a replacement for peer review?
No. Treat it as a first-pass red team before you submit: a way to catch the obvious problems a real reviewer will flag before they do. The review is advisory only; you remain responsible for every decision about your manuscript.
What if the review misses something or makes a mistake?
AI reviewers can be wrong. The output flags concerns with hedged language and quotes the manuscript when challenging a claim, so you can verify every finding against your text. Not useful? Email ben@purplelink.llc within 14 days of purchase and it is refunded. One refund per manuscript.
What file format?
PDF up to 20 MB. If you're submitting LaTeX, compile to PDF first (our free LaTeX-to-PDF tool works).
What about clinical or patient data?
Do not upload identifiable patient data, data covered by a data use agreement, or export-controlled material. The manuscript is sent to Anthropic's API, which keeps it for up to 30 days and does not train on it. For papers containing identifiable health information, redact before upload.
