## Option 1

I almost shipped a product screenshot with a compile error in it.

I was going through the ModernTex product page and looked closely at the flagship editor screenshot, the one meant to show the whole point of the app: the editor and PDF preview staying in sync while you write. Instead it showed a real compile error, a stray tab open for a personal file named Kalshi.tex, and a template still reading "Your Paper Title Here."

None of that breaks anything. It is the kind of detail you stop seeing once you have looked at a page fifty times. It is also exactly the kind of detail a researcher deciding whether to trust a new ten dollar editor with their manuscript would notice in the first three seconds.

So I reshot it. A clean, zero error compile of the app's own bundled MISQ template, fictional title and abstract, editor and PDF preview lined up the way they are supposed to be, captured at the exact pixel dimensions the site requires.

It is a small fix and it will not show up in a changelog anyone reads twice. But ModernTex exists so a manuscript looks right before it goes out the door, and the page selling it should hold itself to the same standard. Current version is up at https://purplelink.llc/moderntex/.

If you have caught something this small and this embarrassing in your own product right before it shipped, I would like to hear what it was.

## Option 2

This week I published a comparison guide that argues against my own product in three places.

The page is "AI paper review tools compared," covering Paperpal Preflight, SciScore, Writefull, a general AI chatbot, and Paper Review, which is mine. I open with a disclosure that I make one of the five tools compared, then try to write every entry the way I would want to read it if I were the one choosing, not the way that flatters mine.

That meant saying plainly that SciScore does a narrower, deeper job than Paper Review for biomedical methods sections, and that a computer science or social science paper gives SciScore almost nothing to check. It meant saying Paperpal Preflight is bundled into a subscription some researchers already pay for, so it may cost them nothing extra, while Paper Review is always an added purchase. It meant admitting a general chatbot can produce a useful read in minutes, and that its real weakness is not laziness, it is that a long manuscript gets read unevenly and references are not checked against a bibliographic database unless the assistant does that lookup for every single one.

Writing the disclosure was easy. Writing the parts where a competitor wins was the actual test of whether the page was honest or just looked honest. It is live at https://purplelink.llc/guides/ai-paper-review-tools-compared/.

I would be glad to hear about a fifth or sixth tool in this space that belongs in the comparison.

## Option 3

Last week's site audit turned up a caching bug that had been live for a while. Fonts and vendor scripts were supposed to cache for a year, but they were only caching for thirty days.

The cause was ordering. Netlify applies the last matching header rule in netlify.toml to a given file, not the most specific one. My immutable-for-a-year rule for fonts and vendor assets sat above the general assets rule, so the general rule's thirty day policy was quietly winning for every font and vendor file on the site. I confirmed it with curl before touching anything, moved the general rule above the specific ones, and confirmed the fix the same way.

The same pass fixed three product pages that preloaded a PNG at high priority while the actual image on the page rendered as WebP, downloading both files for nothing. It also replaced the raster nav logo with a hand traced SVG built from its own alpha mask and run through SVGO: 2.2 KB against the 2.5 KB PNG it replaced, and sharp at any zoom level instead of blurry past a certain size.

None of this is visible to a visitor. It shows up only as a page that loads slightly faster and weighs slightly less. That is most of what performance work actually is: proving something is wrong, changing one thing, proving it is fixed.

I would like to hear about the least visible fix you shipped that you were still quietly proud of.

## Selected
Option 1 -- it is the most concrete and honest of the three: a specific, slightly embarrassing real mistake (a live compile error and a personal file name visible on the flagship product page) caught and fixed, and it speaks directly to why a researcher would trust ModernTex with a manuscript.
