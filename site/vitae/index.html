Vitae in short

Vitae is a free Mac app for academics that keeps your submissions, grants, projects, co-authors, teaching, service and CV in one record on your own Mac, with no account. It needs macOS 15 or later. An optional subscription, Vitae Plus, costs $2.99 a month or $23.99 a year and adds reports and Word export. Vitae does not fill in your university's reporting system for you, and it has no iPhone, web or Windows version.

What's inside

Everything an academic needs, nothing they don't

Submission tracking

Log every journal and conference submission with status, dates, each review round, and the decision. Pick target venues with published acceptance rates for about 13,700 journals and conferences, and get a forecast of publication timelines from your own submission history.

Grant management

Track NSF, NIH, and other grants from planning to award: deadlines, requested and awarded amounts, reporting dates, and your share. Search Grants.gov for open funding opportunities from inside the app.

CV & packages

Start from the CV you have. Vitae imports it from Word, PDF, RTF, ODT, or a web page, in APA, Chicago, MLA, IEEE, ACM, Harvard, or Vancouver style. It reads your papers, grants, positions, talks, teaching and service, and shows you everything before saving. From then on your CV fills itself from your records. Choose from seven CV formats or build your own, and export several at once to PDF, Word, rich text, HTML or Markdown. Word (.docx) templates are part of Vitae Plus.

Inbox triage

Connect iCloud, Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo, Fastmail, or your university mail, read-only. Vitae recognizes messages from journal submission systems, reads them on your Mac, and proposes submission updates. You review each one before anything changes.

Collaborators, projects & ideas

Track coauthors, collaborators, and projects. Link people to papers, grants, and roles so your network stays navigable. Capture research ideas before they are projects. Sort them on a board by stage, group them by type or author, plot them by impact against feasibility, or link them on a map. Then make one a project with its authors in order. Send any publication to Zotero in one click.

For You, topics and the map

Vitae builds a map of how your papers, co-authors, venues, grants and ideas connect, on your Mac, and a few plain rules point out what may need you: a decision taking longer than usual at a venue, an annual review with details missing, a grant about to end. Each item lists the records behind it. Papers also get up to three research topics from the OpenAlex list, which you can change, and co-author affiliations are matched to institutions for a collaboration map and a research timeline. Some of this needs Vitae Plus.

Local-first & private

Everything is stored on your Mac with SwiftData. No account and no cloud sync required, and keeping your record needs no subscription. Vitae takes a daily backup and lets you undo changes, so a bad import or merge is never permanent.

Submissions, CV, grants and tenure

Every screen uses sample data.

  1. Every submission, every round

    One table holds the whole history: venue, date submitted, decision, and your own estimate of the odds. Open a submission to see its review rounds, manuscript versions, cover letter and submission checklist.

  2. A CV built from your records

    Pick a format, a length and a date range, and the CV fills itself from the papers, positions and grants you already track. Export to PDF, Word, rich text, HTML or Markdown.

  3. Grants from proposal to final report

    Requested and awarded amounts, your share, the project timeline and each reporting deadline. Awarded and pending totals sit at the top of the list.

  4. How close you are to tenure

    Set your tenure date and your department’s expectations, and Vitae counts your record against each one: articles, lead-author papers, funding, h-index, invited talks and service.

Frequently asked questions

Is Vitae really free?
Yes. Vitae is free to download and use, with all core record-keeping and no limits, and nothing that was free before Plus becomes paid. An optional subscription, Vitae Plus ($2.99 a month or $23.99 a year, with a 7-day free trial), adds newer tools: reports such as an NIH biosketch draft, Word export with your own templates, the revision workspace, Shareables, the collaboration map, batch DOI lookup and ORCID sync, Reminders and calendar sync, and themes. It is also included for life in the Mac Suite. You do not need it to keep your record. A tip via Buy Me a Coffee is also appreciated but never required.
Does Vitae show ads?
Not from anyone else. The sidebar has one small card, labelled "From Purplelink", that suggests another Purplelink tool such as ModernTex, Paper Review or Vitae Plus. If a sponsor ever appears there, the card will say "Sponsor" and the sponsor's name, and this page will say so. There is no ad network, no tracking, and no click reporting, and Vitae sends nothing to sponsors. Vitae reads the card's text from a plain file on purplelink.llc at most once a day. It shows a copy built into the app when you are offline. You can hide the card in Settings, under Support. Vitae then stops fetching it.
How does Vitae update?
Vitae checks purplelink.llc once a day and asks before installing a new version. Updates are signed, and Vitae refuses any that fail the check. You can turn the daily check off, or check by hand, in Settings under About. Version 0.1, the first release, cannot update itself. If you still have it, download the current version once from this page and it will keep itself up to date after that.
Why isn't it on the Mac App Store?
Distributing outside the App Store lets me ship faster and avoid App Store restrictions. The DMG is Apple-notarized, so macOS Gatekeeper verifies it is safe to run. Just open the DMG, drag Vitae to Applications, and launch it.
What macOS version is required?
Vitae requires macOS 15 Sequoia or later.
Where is my data stored?
All your records are stored locally on your Mac using SwiftData, with no account. Vitae goes online for lookups you ask for, such as citation counts from Crossref or OpenAlex and Grants.gov funding searches, and reads your email only if you connect a mailbox, read-only. It also makes two small requests to purplelink.llc on its own. One is the daily update check. The other is the daily fetch of the sidebar card's text, unless you hide the card. Neither sends anything about you or your records. Vitae Plus can sync your library between your Macs through your private iCloud. It is off until you turn it on in Settings, Backups, and private fields are encrypted.
How do I install it?
Download the DMG, open it, drag Vitae into your Applications folder, and launch it. Because it is notarized by Apple, you will not see a Gatekeeper warning.
Does Vitae import into Faculty180 or Faculty Success?
Vitae exports your publications as RIS and BibTeX files, free, and its export menu says they import into Interfolio and Watermark Faculty Success. I have not tested a Vitae file against a live Faculty180 or Faculty Success account, and campuses switch imports on and off. For Faculty180 the BibTeX file is the safer choice. The guides on this site give the steps for each system.
Can Vitae do my annual report?
Partly. Annual Review mode in CV & Packages builds a report for a calendar year, an academic year (you choose the start month) or custom dates, from your record, and exports it, free. It lists the period's publications, grants active in it, awards, talks, teaching, service and advising, and leaves papers under review out. The free export folder holds the period's RIS and BibTeX files. Vitae Plus adds a faculty activity Word file, a check for missing details and a README. Vitae does not fill in your department's form or your university's reporting system, so you copy the content across or import the files.
Is Vitae the same as the Chronicle's Vitae?
No. This Vitae is a Mac app made by Purplelink LLC. The Chronicle of Higher Education launched a separate career hub with the same name in 2014.
How does Vitae compare to Interfolio, ORCID, or a spreadsheet?
Vitae keeps your own record between deadlines. If your university requires Interfolio or Watermark for tenure review, you still submit there, using what Vitae exports.
